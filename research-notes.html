Matching away the Taylor complex of (xy, yz, zw, xw)

The Taylor complex of I = (xy, yz, zw, xw) is the full simplex on the four generators, where each face is labeled by the lcm of its vertices. Its ranks are 1, 4, 6, 4, 1, but the minimal free resolution of S/I has ranks 1, 4, 4, 1. All of the extra faces have multidegree xyzw.

A matching here is a set of disjoint pairs (σ, τ) of faces, where σ is a facet of τ and both have the same lcm. We use the matching {(xy·yz·zw, whole simplex), (xy·zw, xy·zw·xw), (yz·xw, yz·zw·xw)}, writing a face as the product of its vertices. It's acyclic, meaning there is no closed path that goes up along matched pairs and down along facets, and that is what lets us cancel the pairs one at a time. Cancelling (σ, τ) is pictured as pushing σ through τ until it lands on the rest of the boundary of τ.

The triangle xy·yz·xw is the only matched-degree face left unmatched. When the edge yz·xw gets pushed away, this triangle gets dragged along with it, and at the end it's a disk glued along the 4-cycle xy, yz, zw, xw. That disk is the last syzygy of the minimal resolution.
