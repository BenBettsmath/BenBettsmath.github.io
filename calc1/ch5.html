Chapter 5 · Applications of Integration

← previousoutlinenext →

The definite integral adds up many small pieces. Every application in this chapter follows one pattern: cut the quantity into thin slices, approximate one slice by something simple (a rectangle, a disk, a constant force), and integrate. Area between curves, volume, work, and average value are four instances.

5.1 Areas Between Curves

SlicesLet $f$ and $g$ be continuous with $f(x) \ge g(x)$ on $[a, b]$. The region between $y = f(x)$ (top) and $y = g(x)$ (bottom) from $x = a$ to $x = b$ has area

$\displaystyle A = \int_a^b \big[f(x) - g(x)\big]\,dx = \int_a^b (\text{top} - \text{bottom})\,dx.$

Why. A vertical strip at $x$ of width $\Delta x$ is nearly a rectangle of height $f(x) - g(x)$. Adding the strips is a Riemann sum, and its limit is the integral. The height $f - g$ is correct even when the curves dip below the $x$-axis.

Example. The region between $y = 3 + 2x - x^2$ and $y = x + 1$. The curves meet where $3 + 2x - x^2 = x + 1$, i.e. $x^2 - x - 2 = (x - 2)(x + 1) = 0$, so $x = -1$ and $x = 2$. Testing $x = 0$ shows the parabola is on top. So

$\displaystyle A = \int_{-1}^{2}\big(2 + x - x^2\big)\,dx = \Big[2x + \tfrac{x^2}{2} - \tfrac{x^3}{3}\Big]_{-1}^{2} = \tfrac{10}{3} - \left(-\tfrac76\right) = \tfrac92.$
One strip, then all of them. The yellow strip has height $(3 + 2x - x^2) - (x + 1) = 2 + x - x^2$. Integrating that height from $x = -1$ to $x = 2$ gives the area $\tfrac92$.

Curves that cross. If the top and bottom curves switch, split the interval at the crossing points, or integrate $|f(x) - g(x)|$. Example: between $y = x^2$ and $y = 2x$ on $[0, 3]$, the line is on top on $[0, 2]$ and the parabola on $[2, 3]$, so $A = \int_0^2(2x - x^2)\,dx + \int_2^3(x^2 - 2x)\,dx = \tfrac43 + \tfrac43 = \tfrac83$.

Horizontal strips. If the region is bounded by curves $x = f(y)$ (right) and $x = g(y)$ (left) for $c \le y \le d$, then

$\displaystyle A = \int_c^d\big[f(y) - g(y)\big]\,dy = \int_c^d (\text{right} - \text{left})\,dy.$

Example. Between $x = y^2$ and $x = y + 6$: they meet where $y^2 - y - 6 = (y - 3)(y + 2) = 0$, so $y = -2$ and $y = 3$, and the line is on the right in between. So $A = \int_{-2}^{3}(y + 6 - y^2)\,dy = \tfrac{125}{6}$. With vertical strips the bottom boundary changes formula at $x = 4$ (from $y = -\sqrt x$ to $y = x - 6$), so that way needs two integrals.

Common pitfall

Sketch first. The limits come from the intersection points, and the order (top minus bottom, or right minus left) from the picture. A negative answer means the order was wrong. Choose $dy$ when the left and right boundaries are each a single curve but the top or bottom changes formula.

5.2 Volumes

SlicesSlice a solid by planes perpendicular to the $x$-axis. If the slice at $x$ has area $A(x)$, a slab of thickness $\Delta x$ has volume about $A(x)\,\Delta x$, so the solid between $x = a$ and $x = b$ has volume

$\displaystyle V = \int_a^b A(x)\,dx.$

Example (pyramid). A pyramid with a square base of side $b$ and height $h$. Measure $x$ down from the apex. By similar triangles the slice at $x$ is a square of side $\tfrac{b}{h}x$, so $A(x) = \tfrac{b^2}{h^2}x^2$ and $V = \int_0^h\tfrac{b^2}{h^2}x^2\,dx = \tfrac13 b^2 h$.

Solids of revolution: disks. Rotate the region under $y = f(x) \ge 0$, $a \le x \le b$, about the $x$-axis. Each slice is a disk of radius $R(x) = f(x)$, so $A(x) = \pi R(x)^2$ and

$\displaystyle V = \int_a^b \pi\,[R(x)]^2\,dx.$

Example: rotating the region under $y = \sqrt x$, $0 \le x \le 4$, about the $x$-axis gives $V = \int_0^4\pi x\,dx = 8\pi$.

Washers. If the region lies between an outer curve and an inner curve, each slice is a disk with a hole, a washer, with outer radius $R(x)$ and inner radius $r(x)$ measured from the axis of rotation:

$\displaystyle V = \int_a^b \pi\Big([R(x)]^2 - [r(x)]^2\Big)\,dx.$

Example. The region between $y = x$ and $y = x^2$ ($0 \le x \le 1$) rotated about the $x$-axis: $R = x$, $r = x^2$, and $V = \pi\int_0^1(x^2 - x^4)\,dx = \pi\left(\tfrac13 - \tfrac15\right) = \tfrac{2\pi}{15}$. About the line $y = -1$ instead, each radius gains $1$: $R = x + 1$, $r = x^2 + 1$, and $V = \pi\int_0^1\big[(x + 1)^2 - (x^2 + 1)^2\big]\,dx = \pi\int_0^1(2x - x^2 - x^4)\,dx = \tfrac{7\pi}{15}$.

A washer slice. The region between $y = x$ (outer) and $y = x^2$ (inner) spun about a horizontal axis (purple). The slice at $x$ is a ring with area $\pi(R^2 - r^2)$. Switching the axis to $y = -1$ adds $1$ to both radii.

Rotating about a vertical line. Slice perpendicular to that line, i.e. horizontally, and write the radii as functions of $y$. Example: the same region rotated about the $y$-axis. For $0 \le y \le 1$ the region runs from $x = y$ (from $y = x$) to $x = \sqrt y$ (from $y = x^2$), so $R(y) = \sqrt y$, $r(y) = y$, and $V = \pi\int_0^1(y - y^2)\,dy = \tfrac{\pi}{6}$.

Radius from an axis

A radius is a distance from the axis, so it is always (farther value) minus (axis value). About $y = c$ with the region above the axis, $R = (\text{outer } y) - c$. About $y = 4$ with the region below the axis, $R = 4 - (\text{outer } y)$, where "outer" now means the curve farther from $y = 4$.

Common pitfall

$\pi(R^2 - r^2)$ is not $\pi(R - r)^2$. Square each radius separately, then subtract. Also match the variable to the slices: slices perpendicular to the $x$-axis use $dx$ and radii in terms of $x$, and slices perpendicular to the $y$-axis use $dy$ and radii in terms of $y$.

5.3 Work

PhysicsA constant force $F$ moving an object a distance $d$ along its line does work $W = Fd$. Units: newton-meters, called joules (J), or foot-pounds (ft-lb). If the force varies with position, $F = F(x)$, then on a short piece $[x_{i - 1}, x_i]$ it is nearly constant, the work there is about $F(x_i^*)\,\Delta x$, and

$\displaystyle W = \int_a^b F(x)\,dx.$

Example: a force $F(x) = 3x^2$ newtons moving an object from $x = 1$ m to $x = 2$ m does $W = \int_1^2 3x^2\,dx = 7$ J.

Hooke's Law. The force needed to hold a spring stretched $x$ units beyond its natural length is $F(x) = kx$, where $k > 0$ is the spring constant. Here $x$ is the stretch, not the length.

Example. A force of $10$ N holds a spring stretched from its natural length $20$ cm to $25$ cm. Then $10 = k(0.05)$, so $k = 200$ N/m. The work to stretch it from $25$ cm to $30$ cm is

$\displaystyle W = \int_{0.05}^{0.10}200x\,dx = \Big[100x^2\Big]_{0.05}^{0.10} = 1 - 0.25 = 0.75\ \text{J}.$
Work is area under the force graph. A spring with $k = 30$ N/m. Stretching it $x$ meters takes $W = \int_0^x 30s\,ds = 15x^2$ J, the triangle under $F = 30s$. Each extra centimeter costs more than the last.

A leaking bucket. A bucket of water weighing $40$ N is lifted $10$ m at constant speed while it leaks, so that its weight drops steadily to $20$ N at the top. At height $x$ the weight is $F(x) = 40 - 2x$ N, so $W = \int_0^{10}(40 - 2x)\,dx = 400 - 100 = 300$ J. When different pieces of an object move different distances (a hanging chain, liquid pumped from a tank), slice the object instead and add (weight of a slice) times (distance that slice moves).

Common pitfall

In Hooke's Law, convert lengths to stretches by subtracting the natural length first, and keep units consistent (meters with newtons gives joules). Stretching from $25$ cm to $30$ cm means $x$ from $0.05$ to $0.10$ m, not from $0.25$ to $0.30$.

5.4 Average Value of a Function

MeanThe average of $n$ numbers $y_1, \ldots, y_n$ is $\tfrac{1}{n}(y_1 + \cdots + y_n)$. Sampling $f$ at $n$ equally spaced points of $[a, b]$ and writing $\tfrac1n = \tfrac{\Delta x}{b - a}$ turns this average into $\tfrac{1}{b - a}\sum f(x_i)\Delta x$, a Riemann sum divided by $b - a$. Letting $n \to \infty$, the average value of $f$ on $[a, b]$ is

$\displaystyle f_{\text{ave}} = \frac{1}{b - a}\int_a^b f(x)\,dx.$

Picture. $f_{\text{ave}}$ is the height of the rectangle on $[a, b]$ with the same area as the region under $f$. The part of the region above the line $y = f_{\text{ave}}$ exactly fills the gaps below it.

Leveling off. $f(x) = 3x - x^2$ on $[0, 3]$. Blue is where $f$ is above the level $h$, pink where it is below. $\int_0^3(f - h)\,dx = 0$ exactly when $h = f_{\text{ave}} = \tfrac32$.

Example. For $f(x) = 3x - x^2$ on $[0, 3]$, $\int_0^3(3x - x^2)\,dx = \tfrac{27}{2} - 9 = \tfrac92$, so $f_{\text{ave}} = \tfrac13\cdot\tfrac92 = \tfrac32$.

Mean Value Theorem for Integrals
If $f$ is continuous on $[a, b]$, then there is a number $c$ in $[a, b]$ with $f(c) = f_{\text{ave}}$, that is, $\displaystyle\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = f(c)(b - a)$.

Why. $f_{\text{ave}}$ lies between the minimum and maximum values of $f$ on $[a, b]$ (by the comparison property of 4.2), so the IVT gives a $c$ where $f$ takes that value. In the example, $3c - c^2 = \tfrac32$ gives $c = \tfrac{3 \pm \sqrt 3}{2}$, both in $[0, 3]$.

Common pitfall

The average value of $f$ is not $\tfrac{f(a) + f(b)}{2}$, and it is not the average rate of change $\tfrac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}$. Remember the factor $\tfrac{1}{b - a}$ in front of the integral.

Worked example

One region, three questions. Let $\mathcal R$ be the region between $y = 2\sqrt x$ and $y = x$. The curves meet where $2\sqrt x = x$, i.e. $x = 0$ and $x = 4$, with $2\sqrt x$ on top in between.
Area: $\displaystyle\int_0^4\left(2\sqrt x - x\right)dx = \Big[\tfrac43 x^{3/2} - \tfrac{x^2}{2}\Big]_0^4 = \tfrac{32}{3} - 8 = \tfrac83$.
Volume about the $x$-axis: $R = 2\sqrt x$, $r = x$, so $V = \pi\displaystyle\int_0^4\left(4x - x^2\right)dx = \pi\left(32 - \tfrac{64}{3}\right) = \tfrac{32\pi}{3}$.
Volume about $y = -1$: $R = 2\sqrt x + 1$, $r = x + 1$, so $V = \pi\displaystyle\int_0^4\Big[(2\sqrt x + 1)^2 - (x + 1)^2\Big]dx = \pi\int_0^4\big(2x + 4\sqrt x - x^2\big)\,dx = \pi\left(16 + \tfrac{64}{3} - \tfrac{64}{3}\right) = 16\pi$.

Practice problems

  1. Sketch and find the area of the region bounded by $y = x^2 - 2x$ and $y = x$.
  2. Find the area between $x = y^2 - 1$ and $x = 1 - y^2$ using horizontal strips.
  3. Find the area between $y = x^3$ and $y = x$ on $[-1, 1]$. (The curves cross.)
  4. Rotate the region under $y = 3 - x$, $0 \le x \le 3$, about the $x$-axis. Find the volume, and check it against the cone formula $V = \tfrac13\pi r^2 h$.
  5. Set up (do not evaluate) the volume when the region between $y = x^2$ and $y = 4$ is rotated about the $y$-axis, about $y = 4$, and about $y = -1$.
  6. A force of $8$ N holds a spring stretched $0.2$ m beyond its natural length. How much work stretches it from $0.2$ m to $0.5$ m beyond natural length?
  7. Find the average value of $f(x) = \cos x$ on $[0, \pi/2]$, and of $f(x) = x^2$ on $[0, 6]$. Find every $c$ the Mean Value Theorem for Integrals promises for the second.

Strategies

Always sketch. The picture gives the intersection points (limits), which curve is on top or outside, and whether to slice with $dx$ or $dy$.

Area. $\int(\text{top} - \text{bottom})\,dx$ or $\int(\text{right} - \text{left})\,dy$. Split where the curves cross.

Volume of revolution. Slice perpendicular to the axis. Disk: $\pi R^2$. Washer: $\pi(R^2 - r^2)$. Radii are distances from the axis, so shift them when the axis is not a coordinate axis.

Work. Force times distance on a small piece, then integrate. Springs: find $k$ from the given data first, using stretches.

Average value. Integrate and divide by the length of the interval. To find $c$, solve $f(c) = f_{\text{ave}}$ and keep the solutions in $[a, b]$.
