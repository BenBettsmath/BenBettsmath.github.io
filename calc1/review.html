1. Formulas to know

The definitions everything else is built from. Each one is a sentence you should be able to write and also a picture you should be able to draw.

Problem 1(a). State the definition of $f$ is continuous at $x=a$.

Problem 1(b). Write $f'(a)$, the derivative of $f$ at $x=a$, as a limit in two ways.

Problem 1(c). Give both parts of the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.

2. Limits from a graph

A limit asks where the graph is heading, not where the dot is. Cover the point $x=a$ with your thumb and read the heights on each side.

Problem 2(a). Let $g$ be the function graphed below (a filled dot is a point on the graph, an open dot is not). Find $g(-1)$, $\lim_{x \to -1^-} g(x)$, $\lim_{x \to -1^+} g(x)$, $\lim_{x \to -1} g(x)$, then $g(0)$ and $\lim_{x \to 0} g(x)$, then $\lim_{x \to 2^-} g(x)$, $\lim_{x \to 2^+} g(x)$, $\lim_{x \to 2} g(x)$.

The graph of $g$. Open dots are not on the graph, filled dots are.

Problem 2(b). At which $x$ values in $(-3,4)$ is $g$ not continuous? Classify each discontinuity. At which of these is $g$ continuous from the left, or from the right?

Problem 2(c). Suppose $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = 4$ and $\lim_{x \to a} h(x) = -2$. Evaluate $\lim_{x \to a} [3f(x) - 5h(x)]$, $\lim_{x \to a} f(x)h(x)$, $\lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x)}{h(x)}$, and $\lim_{x \to a} \parens{\sqrt{f(x)} + h(x)^2}$.

3. Computing limits

If plugging in gives a number, that is the limit. If it gives $\frac{0}{0}$, the expression is hiding a common factor, so factor, rationalize, or combine fractions until it cancels. If it gives (nonzero)$/0$, the limit is infinite on each side and only the signs are left to decide.

Problem 3(a). Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 3} \frac{x^2-2x-3}{x^2-9}$.

Problem 3(b). Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 5} \frac{\sqrt{x+4}-3}{x-5}$.

Problem 3(c). Evaluate $\lim_{x \to -1} \frac{\frac{1}{x+3} - \frac{1}{2}}{x+1}$.

Problem 3(d). Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 4} \frac{5}{x^2-3x-4}$, considering the one-sided limits.

Problem 3(e). Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 2} \frac{x^2-4}{\abs{x-2}}$.

4. Squeeze Theorem

If $f$ is trapped between two functions that head to the same height $L$, then $f$ has nowhere else to go.

Problem 4(a). Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 0} x^2 \sin\parens{\frac{3}{x}}$.

Problem 4(b). If $2x - 1 \leq f(x) \leq x^2 - 2x + 3$ for all $x$, find $\lim_{x \to 2} f(x)$.

5. Limits at infinity

As $x \to \pm\infty$ only the biggest power matters. Divide top and bottom by the highest power of $x$ in the bottom.

Problem 5(a). Evaluate $\lim_{x \to -\infty} \frac{\sqrt{4x^2+1}}{x+3}$.

Problem 5(b). Evaluate $\lim_{x \to \infty} \parens{\sqrt{x^2+6x} - x}$.

Problem 5(c). Find all vertical and horizontal asymptotes of $f(x) = \frac{2x^2-2}{x^2+x-2}$. Why is $x=1$ not a vertical asymptote?

6. Continuity and the IVT

To check continuity at a point compute three things: the value, the left limit, the right limit. All three must agree.

Problem 6(a). Let $f(x) = \begin{cases} x+2 & x < -1 \\ x^2 & -1 \leq x \leq 1 \\ 3-x & x > 1 \end{cases}$. Is $f$ continuous at $x=-1$ and at $x=1$? Where is $f$ not differentiable?

Problem 6(b). Find $a$ and $b$ so that $f(x) = \begin{cases} 2 & x < 1 \\ ax + b & 1 \leq x < 4 \\ 8 & x \geq 4 \end{cases}$ is continuous everywhere.

Problem 6(c). Show that $x^3 + x - 1 = 0$ has a solution in $(0,1)$.

7. The derivative as a limit

The tangent slope is the limit of secant slopes. Every limit-definition problem has the same shape: write the difference quotient, simplify until the $h$ in the bottom cancels, then let $h\to0$.

Problem 7(a). Let $f(x) = x^3$ and $P = (2,8)$. Find the slope of the tangent line at $P$ as a limit of slopes of secant lines through $P$ and $Q = (x, x^3)$, then write the tangent line.

Problem 7(b). Compute $f'(x)$ directly from $f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}$ for $f(x) = \frac{x}{x+2}$.

Problem 7(c). A ball's height is $h(t) = 200 - 16t^2$ feet after $t$ seconds. Find the slope of the secant line between $t=1$ and $t=3$, then the instantaneous velocity at $t=2$ as a limit.

Problem 7(d). Show that $f(x) = \sqrt[3]{x}$ is continuous at $x=0$ but not differentiable there. What does the tangent line at $x=0$ look like?

Problem 7(e). Each limit is $f'(a)$ for some $f$ and $a$. Find $f$ and $a$, then evaluate: $\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{(2+h)^4 - 16}{h}$ and $\lim_{x \to \pi/2} \frac{\cos x}{x - \frac{\pi}{2}}$.

8. Differentiation rules

Problem 8(a). Find $y'$ if $y = \frac{x^2+1}{2x+1}$.

Problem 8(b). Find the points on the curve $y = x^3 - 3x^2 - 9x + 2$ where the tangent line is parallel to the line $y = 15x$.

Problem 8(c). A particle has position $s(t) = t^3 - 9t^2 + 24t$ meters at time $t$ seconds. Find $v(t)$ and $a(t)$, with units. When is the particle at rest?

9. Trig derivatives

Every trig limit in this course reduces to $\lim_{\theta\to0}\frac{\sin\theta}{\theta} = 1$. The trick is to make the angle inside the sine match the denominator exactly.

Problem 9(a). Starting only from $\frac{d}{dx}\sin x = \cos x$ and $\frac{d}{dx}\cos x = -\sin x$, derive $\frac{d}{dx}\sec x$. Then find $\frac{d^2}{dx^2}\tan x$.

Problem 9(b). Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(4x)}{3x}$ and $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1-\cos x}{x\sin x}$.

Problem 9(c). Find $y'$ if $y = \frac{\sec x}{1 + \tan x}$.

10. Chain rule

The chain rule says rates multiply: if $u$ changes $3$ times as fast as $x$ and $y$ changes $2$ times as fast as $u$, then $y$ changes $6$ times as fast as $x$. In symbols, $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du}\frac{du}{dx}$.

Problem 10(a). Use the table to find $(f \circ g)'(1)$, $(g \circ f)'(1)$, and $(fg)'(2)$. $$\begin{array}{c|cccc} x & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 \\ \hline f(x) & 2 & 3 & 0 & 1 \\ f'(x) & -1 & 4 & 2 & 5 \\ g(x) & 1 & 2 & 3 & 0 \\ g'(x) & 3 & -2 & 1 & 4 \end{array}$$

Problem 10(b). Find the tangent line to $y = \tan(\sqrt{x})$ at $x = \frac{\pi^2}{16}$.

Problem 10(c). Find the tangent line to $y = (x^2-3)^5$ at $x = 2$.

Problem 10(d). Find all points $(x,y)$ with $0 \leq x < 2\pi$ where $y = 2\sin x + \cos(2x)$ has a horizontal tangent line.

11. Implicit differentiation

Treat $y$ as an unknown function of $x$. Every time you differentiate something with $y$ in it, the chain rule leaves a factor of $y'$. Then solve for $y'$.

Problem 11(a). For the ellipse $x^2 + 4y^2 = 16$, find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ and $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$, simplified using the equation of the ellipse.

Problem 11(b). Find the tangent line to $\sin(x+y) = 2x - 2y$ at $(\pi, \pi)$.

Problem 11(c). Find the tangent line to $x^3 + y^3 = 9xy$ at $(2,4)$.

Problem 11(d). For $y^2 = x^3 - 3x + 2$, find $\frac{dy}{dx}$, the tangent line at $(2,2)$, and all points with a horizontal tangent line.

12. Related rates

Write one equation that holds at every instant, differentiate it with respect to $t$, and only then plug in the numbers for the instant you care about. Plugging in first freezes the quantities and kills their rates.

Problem 12(a). A 13 ft ladder leans against a wall. The bottom slides away at 2 ft/s. How fast is the top sliding down when the bottom is 5 ft from the wall?

Problem 12(b). A tank is an inverted cone with radius 3 m at the top and height 6 m. Water is pumped in at 4 $\text{m}^3$/min. How fast is the water level rising when the water is 4 m deep?

Problem 12(c). A 6 ft tall person walks away from a 15 ft lamppost at 3 ft/s. How fast is the tip of the person's shadow moving?

Problem 12(d). A marble moves along $x^2 + xy + y^2 = 9$. At $(3,0)$, $x$ is decreasing at 2 ft/s. Is $y$ increasing or decreasing, and how fast?

13. Linear approximation

Near $x=a$ a differentiable curve looks like its tangent line, so we use $L(x) = f(a) + f'(a)(x-a)$ in place of $f(x)$.

Problem 13(a). Find the linearization of $f(x) = \sqrt[3]{x}$ at $a=27$. Use it to approximate $\sqrt[3]{27.27}$.

Problem 13(b). Find the linearization of $f(x) = \sin x$ at $a = \frac{\pi}{6}$. Use it to approximate $\sin\parens{\frac{\pi}{6} + 0.02}$.

Problem 13(c). The radius of a sphere is measured as 10 cm with a possible error of 0.05 cm. Estimate the maximum error in the computed volume.

14. Extreme values

A continuous function on a closed interval attains a max and a min (Extreme Value Theorem), and they can only occur at critical numbers or endpoints (Fermat's Theorem). So list those candidates and compare the values of $f$.

Problem 14(a). Find the critical numbers of $f(x) = x^{2/3}(x-5)$. Which come from $f'(x) = 0$ and which from $f'(x)$ undefined?

Problem 14(b). Find the absolute max and min of $f(x) = x^4 - 8x^2 + 3$ on $[-1,3]$.

Problem 14(c). Find the absolute max and min of $f(x) = x\sqrt{4-x^2}$ on $[-1,2]$.

Problem 14(d). Find the absolute max and min of $f(x) = 2\cos x + \sin(2x)$ on $[0, \pi]$.

15. Mean Value Theorem

If $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$ and differentiable on $(a,b)$, then somewhere the tangent slope equals the average slope: $f'(c) = \frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}$ for some $c$ in $(a,b)$. If your average speed on a trip was 60 mph, at some instant the speedometer read exactly 60.

Problem 15(a). Find every $c$ guaranteed by the MVT for $f(x) = x^2 - 4x + 1$ on $[1,5]$.

Problem 15(b). If $f(2) = -1$ and $f'(x) \leq 3$ for all $x$, how large can $f(6)$ be?

Problem 15(c). Show that $x^3 + 5x - 2 = 0$ has exactly one real solution.

16. Shape of a graph

The sign of $f'$ says whether $f$ goes up or down. The sign of $f''$ says whether $f'$ goes up or down, which is whether the graph bends up or down. Find where each is zero or undefined, then test signs between.

Problem 16(a). For $f(x) = x^3 - 6x^2 + 9x + 1$, find the intervals of increase and decrease, local extrema, concavity, and inflection points.

Problem 16(b). Do the same for $f(x) = 3x^4 - 4x^3$.

Problem 16(c). Find constants $a$ and $b$ so that $f(x) = x^3 + ax^2 + bx$ has an inflection point at $x=1$ and a local minimum at $x=3$. Where is its local maximum?

17. Curve sketching

Problem 17(a). Sketch $f(x) = \frac{1}{x^2+3}$, labeling intercepts, asymptotes, extrema, and inflection points.

Problem 17(b). Sketch $f(x) = \frac{x^2}{x-1}$, labeling all key features.

Problem 17(c). Sketch $f(x) = x - 2\sqrt{x}$, labeling all key features.

18. Optimization

Write the quantity as a function of one variable using the constraint, find its critical numbers, and check that the one you pick really is the max or min (endpoints, first derivative test, or second derivative test).

Problem 18(a). A closed box has a rectangular base whose length is twice its width, and its volume is $72\ \text{ft}^3$. Find the dimensions that use the least material.

Problem 18(b). A closed cylindrical can holds $16\pi\ \text{cm}^3$. Find the radius and height that minimize its surface area.

Problem 18(c). A rectangle has its base on the $x$-axis and its two upper corners on the semicircle $y = \sqrt{25 - x^2}$. Find the largest possible area.

Problem 18(d). A rectangular pen is split in half by a divider parallel to one side. The outer fence costs \$4 per meter and the divider \$2 per meter. With \$200, what is the largest possible area?

Problem 18(e). Find the points on the parabola $y = x^2$ closest to the point $(0,2)$.

19. Antiderivatives

An antiderivative undoes a derivative, and it is only determined up to a constant $C$, because constants have derivative $0$. An initial condition picks out the one value of $C$ that fits.

Problem 19(a). Find the most general antiderivative of $g(x) = \frac{x^2 - 3x + 2\sqrt{x}}{x}$.

Problem 19(b). If $f'(x) = 3\sqrt{x} - 2$ and $f(4) = 10$, find $f(x)$.

Problem 19(c). A particle has $a(t) = 6t - 4$, $v(0) = 3$, $s(0) = 1$. Find $s(t)$.

Problem 19(d). Let $F$ be the antiderivative of $f(x) = x^2 - 4$ with $F(0) = 0$. Without finding $F$, decide where $F$ is increasing, where it has local extrema, and where it is concave up. Then find $F$.

20. Riemann sums

A Riemann sum adds up rectangles of width $\Delta x$ and height $f(x_i)$. The definite integral is what these sums approach as the rectangles get thin.

Problem 20(a). Estimate $\int_0^\pi \sin x\, dx$ with the midpoint rule and $n=3$.

Problem 20(b). Approximate the area under $f(x) = x^2$ on $[0,4]$ with $n=4$ using right endpoints, left endpoints, and midpoints. Which are over and which are under?

Problem 20(c). Write $\lim_{n \to \infty} \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{3}{n}\sqrt{1 + \frac{3i}{n}}$ as a definite integral, then evaluate it.

21. The integral as area

$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$ is the area above the axis minus the area below it. For lines and circles we can find these areas with geometry.

Problem 21(a). Evaluate $\int_0^2 \parens{3x + \sqrt{4-x^2}} dx$ with geometry.

Problem 21(b). Evaluate $\int_{-2}^2 \parens{x^3 + \sqrt{4-x^2}} dx$ with geometry.

Problem 21(c). Evaluate $\int_{-3}^3 \parens{\abs{x} - 1} dx$ with geometry.

Problem 21(d). For the velocity graph below ($v$ in m/s, $t$ in s), find the displacement and total distance from $t=0$ to $t=10$, the position at $t=10$ if $x(0)=3$ m, the acceleration at $t=5$, and when the particle is farthest from its start.

Blue area counts forward motion, red area backward motion. Displacement subtracts them, distance adds them.

22. Fundamental Theorem, Part 1

$\frac{d}{dx}\int_a^x f(t)\,dt = f(x)$: the area grows at a rate equal to the height of the graph at the moving edge. When the upper limit is $u(x)$ instead of $x$, the chain rule adds a factor $u'(x)$.

Problem 22(a). Find $g'(x)$ if $g(x) = \int_{x^2}^4 t\sqrt{1+t^2}\, dt$.

Problem 22(b). Find $F'(x)$ if $F(x) = \int_0^{\tan x} \sqrt{1+t^2}\, dt$ for $-\frac{\pi}{2} < x < \frac{\pi}{2}$, and simplify.

Problem 22(c). Find $g'(x)$ if $g(x) = \int_{2x}^{x^3} \frac{1}{1+t^4} dt$.

Problem 22(d). Let $g(x) = \int_0^x f(t) dt$, where $f$ is graphed below. Find $g(4)$, $g'(4)$, $g''(4)$, and where on $[0,6]$ $g$ is largest.

The shaded area is $g(4) = 6$. Past $t = 4$ the area counts negative and $g$ starts to drop.

23. Fundamental Theorem, Part 2

To integrate $f$ over $[a,b]$, find any antiderivative $F$ and compute $F(b) - F(a)$. The constant $C$ cancels, so we leave it out.

Problem 23(a). Evaluate $\int_1^4 \parens{3\sqrt{x} - \frac{2}{x^2}} dx$.

Problem 23(b). Evaluate $\int_0^{\pi} (\sin x + 2\cos x) dx$.

Problem 23(c). What is wrong with $\int_{-1}^2 \frac{1}{x^2} dx = \brac{-\frac{1}{x}}_{-1}^2 = -\frac{3}{2}$?

24. Substitution

Substitution is the chain rule read backward. Look for an inside function $u$ whose derivative $du$ also appears as a factor.

Problem 24(a). Evaluate $\int \sec^2 x \tan^3 x\, dx$.

Problem 24(b). Evaluate $\int_0^{\pi/2} \cos x\sqrt{1 + 3\sin x}\, dx$.

Problem 24(c). Evaluate $\int_{-2}^1 x\sqrt{x+3}\, dx$.

Problem 24(d). Evaluate $\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} x^2 \sin x\, dx$.

25. Area between curves

Area between curves is the integral of (top minus bottom), between the points where the curves meet. If the region is easier to describe left to right, integrate (right minus left) in $y$.

Problem 25(a). Sketch the region between $y = x^2 - 2x$ and $y = x + 4$ and find its area.

The line is on top between $x=-1$ and $x=4$.

Problem 25(b). Find the area between $y = \sqrt{x}$ and $y = \frac{x}{3}$, integrating with respect to $x$, then with respect to $y$.

Problem 25(c). Find the area between $y = \sin x$ and $y = \cos x$ for $0 \leq x \leq \frac{\pi}{2}$.

26. Average value

The average value $f_{\text{ave}} = \frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$ is the height of the rectangle on $[a,b]$ with the same area as the region under $f$.

Problem 26(a). Find the average value of $f(x) = \frac{1}{x^2}$ on $[1,3]$.

Problem 26(b). Find the average value of $f(x) = x^2$ on $[0,3]$ and a $c$ in $[0,3]$ with $f(c)$ equal to it.

27. Volumes

Slice the solid perpendicular to the axis. Each slice is a disk or a washer, with volume $\pi(R^2 - r^2)$ times its thickness, where $R$ is the outer radius and $r$ the inner radius, both measured from the axis.

Problem 27(a). Find the volume when the region between $y = x$ and $y = x^2$ is revolved around the $x$-axis.

Problem 27(b). Find the volume when the region under $y = \sqrt{x}$ for $0 \leq x \leq 4$ is revolved around the $x$-axis.

Problem 27(c). Revolve the region between $y = \sqrt{x}$ and $y = x^2$ around the $y$-axis, and then around the line $x = -1$.

28. Work

Work is force times distance when the force is constant. When it varies, chop the motion into small pieces and add them: $W = \int_a^b F(x)\,dx$. For a spring, Hooke's Law says $F(x) = kx$, where $x$ is the stretch beyond natural length.

Problem 28(a). A spring has natural length 20 cm, and 0.9 J of work stretches it from 20 cm to 26 cm. How much work stretches it from 24 cm to 28 cm? What force holds it at a length of 30 cm?

Problem 28(b). A spring has natural length 10 cm and spring constant $k$. Compare the work $W_1$ from 10 cm to 15 cm with $W_2$ from 15 cm to 20 cm.

Problem 28(c). A 10 m chain with mass 2 kg per meter hangs from the top of a building. How much work does it take to pull the whole chain up to the top? Use $g = 9.8\ \text{m}/\text{s}^2$.

Problem 28(d). A force of $F(x) = \frac{x}{(x^2+1)^2}$ pounds acts on an object $x$ feet from the origin. Find the work done moving the object from $x=0$ to $x=1$.
