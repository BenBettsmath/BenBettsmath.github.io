Group actions

Def. Group action. (D&F §4.1) An action of $G$ on a set $X$ is a homomorphism $G \to \mathrm{Sym}(X)$, equivalently a map $G \times X \to X$ with $1 \cdot x = x$ and $(gh) \cdot x = g \cdot (h \cdot x)$. $G$ permutes $X$, respecting the group structure. The orbit of $x$ is $Gx = \{gx : g \in G\}$; the stabilizer is $G_x = \{g : gx = x\}$, a subgroup of $G$.
Example. $S_n$ acts on $\{1, \ldots, n\}$ (one orbit, stabilizer of $i$ is $S_{n-1}$). $G$ acts on itself by left multiplication (one orbit, trivial stabilizer). By conjugation (orbits are conjugacy classes, stabilizers = centralizers). Three of the most important actions.
Non-example. The trivial action $g \cdot x = x$ is technically valid but useless. A common mistake: writing $g \cdot x = xg^{-1}$ for a left action of $G$. This is actually a *right* action (the formula multiplies on the right). Stick to $g \cdot x = gxg^{-1}$ for conjugation or $gx$ for left multiplication on $G$ itself.
Thm (Orbit-stabilizer). (D&F §4.1, Prop. 2) $$|Gx| = [G:G_x].$$ Size of an orbit equals the index of its stabilizer. Elegant formula underlying all group action applications.
Thm (Class equation). (D&F §4.3, Thm. 7) $$|G| = |Z(G)| + \sum_i [G:C_G(g_i)],$$ the sum running over one representative $g_i$ per conjugacy class of size $>1$. Orbits under conjugation partition $G$. This equation detects nontrivial centers.
Cor. (D&F §4.3, Cor. 9) If $|G| = p^n$ then $Z(G) \neq 1$. $p$-groups always have nontrivial center. Groups of order $p^2$ are abelian.
Thm (Coset action / Cayley). (D&F §4.2, Thm. 3) $G$ acts on the left cosets of $H$ by left multiplication, giving $\phi\colon G \to S_{[G:H]}$. Then $\ker\phi = \text{Core}_G(H) = \bigcap_{g \in G} gHg^{-1}$ (the core of $H$), which is normal in $G$ and contained in $H$. If $[G:H] = n$ and $n! < |G|$, then $\ker\phi$ is nontrivial and proper, giving a nontrivial normal subgroup of $G$.
