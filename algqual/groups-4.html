Abelian groups and the structure theorem

Thm. Structure theorem for finitely generated abelian groups. (D&F §5.2, Thm. 5) Every finitely generated abelian group is isomorphic to $$\mathbb{Z}^r \oplus \mathbb{Z}/d_1\mathbb{Z} \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb{Z}/d_k\mathbb{Z}, \qquad d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \cdots \mid d_k, \quad d_i > 1$$ (invariant factor form), or equivalently as $$\mathbb{Z}^r \oplus \mathbb{Z}/p_1^{a_1}\mathbb{Z} \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb{Z}/p_t^{a_t}\mathbb{Z}$$ (elementary divisor form). One representation per group, in either guise. This is the complete classification. The decomposition is unique in either form.
Example. Order $12$ has two abelian groups: $\mathbb{Z}/12 \cong \mathbb{Z}/4 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3$ (one generator of order $12$) and $\mathbb{Z}/2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/6 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3$ (no element of order $>6$). Different structures, same order.
Non-example. $\mathbb{Z}/4$ and $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$ both have order $4$ but aren't isomorphic. First has an element of order $4$; second doesn't.
Rules. Converting between forms.
  • Invariant factors $\to$ elementary divisors: Factor each $d_i$ into prime powers. Done.
  • Elementary divisors $\to$ invariant factors: Group the prime powers by prime. For each prime $p$, sort its power factors decreasing: $p^{a_1} \geq p^{a_2} \geq \cdots$. The largest invariant factor $d_k = \prod_p p^{a_1(p)}$ (the largest of each prime's list). Then $d_{k-1}$ is the product of each second-largest, and so on. Fill gaps with $1$s. This repackaging is forced: the structure is the same either way.
Computation. Elementary divisors $\{4, 2, 9, 3, 5\}$ over $\mathbb{Z}$.
  • Group by prime: for $p=2$: $\{4, 2\}$. For $p=3$: $\{9, 3\}$. For $p=5$: $\{5\}$.
  • Largest invariant factor: $4 \cdot 9 \cdot 5 = 180$.
  • Second largest: $2 \cdot 3 \cdot 1 = 6$.
  • Check $6 \mid 180$. So $M \cong \mathbb{Z}/6 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/180$.
Prop. Computing $\mathbb{Z}^n/H$ via Smith normal form. (D&F §12.1) Write the generators of $H \leq \mathbb{Z}^n$ as the columns of an $n \times m$ integer matrix $M$, so $M : \mathbb{Z}^m \to \mathbb{Z}^n$ has image $H$ and the quotient is its cokernel, $$0 \to \mathbb{Z}^m \xrightarrow{\ M\ } \mathbb{Z}^n \to \mathbb{Z}^n/H \to 0.$$ Reduce $M$ by integer row and column operations to Smith normal form $\mathrm{diag}(d_1, \ldots, d_r, 0, \ldots, 0)$ with $d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \cdots \mid d_r$. Then $$\mathbb{Z}^n/H \cong \mathbb{Z}/d_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb{Z}/d_r \oplus \mathbb{Z}^{n-r}.$$ Allowed operations are to swap two rows or columns, multiply a row or column by $\pm 1$, and add an integer multiple of one row or column to another. Each zero diagonal entry gives a free $\mathbb{Z}$ summand in the quotient.
Computation. $\mathbb{Z}^4/H$ for $H = \langle (2,2,2,2), (2,4,8,0), (8,8,0,4) \rangle$ (Aug 2022 qual). The generators are the columns of $$M = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 & 8 \\ 2 & 4 & 8 \\ 2 & 8 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix}.$$ Clear the first row off the other columns, $C_2 \leftarrow C_2 - C_1$ and $C_3 \leftarrow C_3 - 4C_1$, then clear the first column with row operations and continue. Padding with a fourth zero column to square it up, this reduces to $\mathrm{diag}(2, 2, 4, 0)$, so the invariant factors are $2, 2, 4$ and the zero pivot is one free direction, $$\mathbb{Z}^4/H \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/4 \oplus \mathbb{Z}.$$
