Structure theorem for f.g. modules over a PID

Thm (Invariant factor form). (D&F §12.1, Thm. 5) Every f.g. module $M$ over a PID $R$ decomposes as $$M \cong R^r \oplus R/(a_1) \oplus \cdots \oplus R/(a_m), \qquad a_1 \mid a_2 \mid \cdots \mid a_m, \quad a_i \in R \setminus R^\times.$$ The $a_i$ are the invariant factors and $r$ is the free rank.
Thm (Elementary divisor form). (D&F §12.1, Thm. 6) Equivalently $M \cong R^r \oplus R/(p_1^{\alpha_1}) \oplus \cdots \oplus R/(p_t^{\alpha_t})$ where the $p_i^{\alpha_i}$ are prime powers (with repetitions allowed). The multiset of elementary divisors is uniquely determined.
Thm (Smith normal form). (D&F §12.1, Thm. 21) Any matrix $A$ over a PID can be put in the form $\mathrm{diag}(d_1, \ldots, d_r, 0, \ldots, 0)$ with $d_1 \mid \cdots \mid d_r$ by elementary row and column operations. For $A = xI - T$ over $k[x]$, the $d_i$ are the invariant factors of the operator $T$.
How to read a quotient as a cokernel. To find $\mathbb{Z}^n/H$ for $H = \langle v_1, \ldots, v_m \rangle$, write generators as the columns of an $n \times m$ matrix $M$. Then $M : \mathbb{Z}^m \to \mathbb{Z}^n$ has image $H$, and the quotient sits in $$0 \to \mathbb{Z}^m \xrightarrow{\ M\ } \mathbb{Z}^n \to \mathbb{Z}^n/H \to 0.$$ Columns, not rows. Row ops change target basis of $\mathbb{Z}^n$, column ops the source basis of $\mathbb{Z}^m$. Each invariant factor $d_i$ gives a cyclic block $\mathbb{Z}/d_i$. A coordinate that no column reaches gives a free $\mathbb{Z}$ summand.
Fun fact. Both forms are the same, seen differently. (D&F §12.1) Invariant factors $a_1 \mid a_2 \mid \cdots \mid a_m$ capture divisibility globally. Elementary divisors are the prime powers in the factorization of the $a_i$. Converting between them is easy: factor each $a_i$ into primes, then collect powers of the same prime. The two forms record the same data in different languages. Invariant factors are better for quotient structure; elementary divisors for Sylow-style analysis by prime.
Two main applications.
  • Abelian groups ($R = \mathbb{Z}$): Every f.g. abelian group is $\mathbb{Z}^r \oplus \mathbb{Z}/d_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb{Z}/d_m$ with $d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \cdots \mid d_m$ (invariant factor form) or a sum of cyclic groups of prime-power order (elementary divisor form, the "canonical form" from group theory).
  • Linear operators ($R = k[x]$): For an operator $T : V \to V$ on a f.d. $k$-vector space, the structure theorem applied to $V$ as a $k[x]$-module (via $x \cdot v = T(v)$) gives invariant factors that yield the Jordan normal form. The cyclic blocks $k[x]/(x - \lambda)^j$ are the generalized eigenspaces.
