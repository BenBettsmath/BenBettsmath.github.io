Rational canonical form

Thm (RCF). (D&F §12.2, Thm. 16-17) Every operator $T$ on an $n$-dimensional $k$-vector space has a unique rational canonical form: a block diagonal $\mathrm{diag}(C_{a_1}, \ldots, C_{a_r})$ with companion matrices for polynomials $a_1 \mid a_2 \mid \cdots \mid a_r$ in $k[x]$. Constraints: $\sum \deg a_i = n$, $\prod a_i = \chi_T$, and $a_r = m_T$. Invariant factors lock down similarity. Two operators similar iff their invariant factor sequences match.
Def. Companion matrix. (D&F §12.2) For monic $f = x^d + b_{d-1} x^{d-1} + \cdots + b_0 \in k[x]$, the companion matrix $C_f$ is the $d \times d$ matrix with $1$'s on the superdiagonal below the main diagonal, last column $(-b_0, -b_1, \ldots, -b_{d-1})^T$, zeros elsewhere. Then $\chi_{C_f} = m_{C_f} = f$. The companion matrix encodes $f$ as an operator.
Fun fact. Rational canonical form is computed in the base field. (D&F §12.2, Cor. 18) Two matrices $A, B$ over $K$ are similar over $K$ if and only if they are similar over any extension field $L \supseteq K$. RCF is purely a polynomial computation in $k[x]$, so extending scalars does not create new similarity classes. Quals love the independence from field extensions.
Thm (Jordan canonical form). (D&F §12.3) Over an algebraically closed field, every operator $T$ is similar to a block diagonal of Jordan blocks $J_k(\lambda) = \lambda I_k + N_k$, where $N_k$ is the $k \times k$ nilpotent shift. One Jordan block per elementary divisor $(x - \lambda)^k$. Over $\mathbb{C}$, Jordan blocks are the canonical form. Over $\mathbb{Q}$ or finite fields, use invariant factors instead.
Prop. Diagonalizability criterion. (D&F §12.3, Prop. 23) An operator $T$ on a vector space over $k$ is diagonalizable if and only if $m_T$ splits into distinct linear factors in $k[x]$. No repeated roots.
Example. If $A^n = I$ and $\gcd(\mathrm{char}(k), n) = 1$, then $m_A \mid x^n - 1$. Since $x^n - 1$ is squarefree (its derivative $nx^{n-1}$ is coprime to it), every divisor is squarefree, so $A$ is diagonalizable over the algebraic closure.
Non-example. A Jordan block $J_2(\lambda)$ has $m = (x-\lambda)^2$, non-squarefree, hence not diagonalizable.
Computation. Classification by invariant factors: $3 \times 3$ matrices over $k$ with $\chi = (x-2)^2(x-3)$.
Invariant factor chains $a_1 \mid a_2$ with product $(x-2)^2(x-3)$ are:
  • $a_1 = 1, a_2 = (x-2)^2(x-3)$: One companion block. RCF is $C_{a_2}$ with $m = a_2$.
  • $a_1 = (x-2), a_2 = (x-2)(x-3)$: Two blocks, $m = a_2 = (x-2)(x-3)$. RCF is $\mathrm{diag}(C_{(x-2)}, C_{(x-2)(x-3)})$.
These are the two similarity classes. Elementary divisor view: $\{(x-2)^2, (x-3)\}$ for class 1 and $\{(x-2), (x-2), (x-3)\}$ for class 2.
Note. Template: matrices with $A^n = I$ over finite fields $\mathbb{F}_q$.
If $A^n = I$ then $m_A \mid x^n - 1$. Steps to enumerate similarity classes:
  1. Factor $x^n - 1$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$ into irreducibles. These correspond to Frobenius orbits of $n$th roots of unity under $\zeta \mapsto \zeta^q$. An orbit's length is the degree of its irreducible factor. Roots of order $d$ have orbit size equal to the order of $q \pmod{d}$.
  2. If $p = \mathrm{char}(\mathbb{F}_q)$ and $p \nmid n$, then $x^n - 1$ is squarefree. Every divisor is too, so all matrices with $A^n = I$ are diagonalizable. Similarity classes correspond to multisets of irreducible factors summing to degree $n$.
  3. If $p \mid n$, write $n = p^s n'$ with $p \nmid n'$. Then $x^n - 1 = (x^{n'}-1)^{p^s}$. Irreducible factors now repeat. Invariant factor chains $a_1 \mid \cdots \mid a_r$ reappear, encoding the nilpotent structure in each Jordan block.
  4. Count by partition type: split $n$ into parts matching degrees of available irreducibles, then count multisets of factors by repetition pattern for each partition.
Computation. Matrices $A$ over $\mathbb{F}_7$ with $A^9 = I$ (Aug 2024 qual).
We have $p = 7$, $n = 9$, $\gcd(7,9) = 1$. So $x^9 - 1$ is squarefree over $\mathbb{F}_7$. Factor into irreducibles as Frobenius orbits of $9$th roots of unity under $\zeta \mapsto \zeta^7$.
  • Order $1$: Root $\zeta = 1$ is fixed by Frobenius, orbit length $1$. Irreducible factor: $x - 1$.
  • Order $3$: The primitive cube roots satisfy $7 \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$, so Frobenius fixes each individually. Two roots, two orbits of length $1$. Over $\mathbb{F}_7$: $2^3 \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$ and $2^2 + 2 + 1 = 7 \equiv 0 \pmod{7}$, so the roots are $2$ and $4$. Factors: $(x-2)(x-4)$.
  • Order $9$: The six primitive $9$th roots. Order of $7 \pmod{9}$ is $3$ (since $7^1 \equiv 7$, $7^2 \equiv 4$, $7^3 \equiv 1 \pmod{9}$). Each orbit has size $3$, giving $6/3 = 2$ irreducible factors of degree $3$.
So $x^9 - 1 = (x-1)(x-2)(x-4)\,f_1 f_2$ over $\mathbb{F}_7$, three linears and two cubics, degrees $1+1+1+3+3 = 9$. Now count. A conjugacy class of $3 \times 3$ matrices is a multiset of elementary divisors with degrees summing to $3$, and since $x^9-1$ is squarefree each elementary divisor is one of these five irreducibles to the first power. Total degree $3$ leaves exactly two shapes.
  • One cubic factor. $2$ classes.
  • Three linear factors chosen from $\{x-1,\, x-2,\, x-4\}$ with repetition allowed. Multisets of size $3$ from $3$ symbols, $\binom{5}{3} = 10$ classes.
Total $10 + 2 = \boxed{12}$ conjugacy classes.
