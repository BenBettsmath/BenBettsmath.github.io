Bonus problems

  1. (Bonus #1) The class equation and what it forces.
    An action of a group $G$ on a set $X$ assigns to each $g \in G$ a permutation $x \mapsto gx$ of $X$, compatibly, so that $(gh)x = g(hx)$ and $ex = x$. The orbit of $x$ is $Gx = \{gx : g \in G\}$ and the stabilizer is $G_x = \{g \in G : gx = x\}$, a subgroup.

    We also use Lagrange's theorem freely. For $H \le G$ the cosets $gH$ partition $G$ into $[G:H]$ sets of size $|H|$, so $|G| = [G:H]\,|H|$.

    The conjugacy class of $x \in G$ is $\{gxg^{-1} : g \in G\}$, its centralizer is $C_G(x) = \{g \in G : gx = xg\}$, and the center is $Z(G) = \{z \in G : zg = gz \text{ for all } g\}$.

    (a) Let $G$ act on itself by conjugation. Show the orbit of $x$ has size $[G:C_G(x)]$, and that the orbit is a single point exactly when $x \in Z(G)$.

    (b) Derive $|G| = |Z(G)| + \sum_i [G:C_G(x_i)]$, the sum over noncentral class representatives.

    (c) Let $|G| = p^n$. Show $p$ divides every term of the sum, and conclude $|Z(G)| \ge p$.

    (d) Show $S_3$ has class sizes $1,2,3$. Explain why the argument in (c) cannot run here.

    (e) Prove that $G/Z(G)$ cyclic forces $G$ abelian.

    (f) Combine (c) and (e) to show $|G| = p^2$ forces $G$ abelian. Then show $|G| = p^3$ does not, using upper unitriangular $3\times3$ matrices over $\mathbb{F}_p$.
  2. (Bonus #2) Cauchy's theorem by a group action.
    Let $p$ divide $|G|$. Set $X = \{(x_1,\dots,x_p) \in G^p : x_1x_2\cdots x_p = e\}$.

    (a) Show $|X| = |G|^{p-1}$.

    (b) Show the cyclic shift $(x_1,\dots,x_p) \mapsto (x_2,\dots,x_p,x_1)$ maps $X$ to $X$ and generates a $\mathbb{Z}/p$ action.

    (c) Show a point of $X$ is fixed exactly when all coordinates are equal.

    (d) Conclude $G$ has an element of order $p$.
  3. (Bonus #3) The isomorphism and correspondence theorems.
    (a) Prove the second isomorphism theorem. For $H \le G$ and $N \trianglelefteq G$, $HN/N \cong H/(H\cap N)$.

    (b) Prove the third. For $N \subseteq K$ both normal in $G$, $(G/N)/(K/N) \cong G/K$.

    (c) State and prove the correspondence between subgroups of $G/N$ and subgroups of $G$ containing $N$.

    (d) Use these to show that if $G/H$ and $G/K$ are nilpotent then so is $G/(H\cap K)$. Here a group $N$ is nilpotent when its lower central series $\gamma_1 = N$, $\gamma_{i+1} = [\gamma_i, N]$ reaches the trivial group.
  4. (Bonus #4) The Sylow counting toolkit.
    Write $|G| = p^am$ with $p \nmid m$. A Sylow $p$-subgroup is a subgroup of order $p^a$, and $\mathrm{Syl}_p(G)$ is the set of them. We take as given Sylow's existence theorem ($\mathrm{Syl}_p(G) \ne \emptyset$) and conjugacy theorem (any two Sylow $p$-subgroups are conjugate). The normalizer of a subgroup $H$ is $N_G(H) = \{g : gHg^{-1} = H\}$. Every count below runs on the orbit stabilizer theorem and Lagrange, both set up in item 1.

    (a) Let $n_p = |\mathrm{Syl}_p(G)|$ denote the number of Sylow $p$-subgroups of $G$. Prove $n_p = [G : N_G(P)]$ for $P$ a Sylow $p$-subgroup.

    (b) Prove $n_p \equiv 1 \bmod p$ by letting $P$ act on $\mathrm{Syl}_p(G)$.

    (c) Prove $n_p$ divides $m$, where $|G| = p^a m$ with $p \nmid m$.

    (d) Use these to show no group of order 132 is simple.

    (e) Run the same count for order 60 and explain precisely why it fails.

    (f) Now suppose $a \ge 2$, so the Sylow $p$-subgroups have order $p^a$ with $p^a > p$. Show that for distinct Sylow $p$-subgroups $P \ne Q$ the intersection $P \cap Q$ is a proper subgroup of $P$, hence has order at most $p^{a-1}$. Then compute how few elements the $n_p$ Sylow $p$-subgroups can contribute in total, in the extreme case where every two of them meet in one common subgroup $K$ of the largest possible order $p^{a-1}$.
  5. (Bonus #5) The coset action, the normal core, and index arguments.
    Let $H \le G$ with $[G:H] = n$.

    (a) Build a homomorphism $\varphi : G \to S_n$ from the left action on $G/H$.

    (b) Show $\ker\varphi = \bigcap_{g\in G} gHg^{-1}$, and that it is the largest normal subgroup of $G$ contained in $H$.

    (c) Conclude $[G : \ker\varphi]$ divides $n!$.

    (d) Now let $n = p$ be the smallest prime dividing $|G|$. Prove $H \trianglelefteq G$.

    (e) Use (c) to show no group of order 36 is simple.
  6. (Bonus #6) The normal core of a subgroup.
    Let $H \le G$. The normal core of $H$ is the intersection of all its conjugates, \[\mathrm{core}_G(H) = \bigcap_{g \in G} gHg^{-1}.\]

    (a) Prove directly, without any coset action, that $\mathrm{core}_G(H)$ is a normal subgroup of $G$, is contained in $H$, and contains every normal subgroup of $G$ that lies in $H$.

    (b) Prove $[G : \mathrm{core}_G(H)]$ divides $[G:H]!$.

    (c) Show a subgroup of index 2 is normal, hence equals its own core. Then compute $\mathrm{core}_{S_3}(\langle(12)\rangle)$.

    (d) Let $P$ be a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $G$. Show $\mathrm{core}_G(P)$ is the intersection of all the Sylow $p$-subgroups, and that it is the largest normal $p$-subgroup of $G$.
  7. (Bonus #7) Groups of order $pq$.
    Let $p < q$ be primes and $|G| = pq$.

    (a) Show the Sylow $q$-subgroup $Q$ is normal.

    (b) Suppose $q \not\equiv 1 \bmod p$. Show the Sylow $p$-subgroup $P$ is normal too.

    (c) Still assuming $q \not\equiv 1 \bmod p$, prove $G$ is cyclic.

    (d) Show that when $q \equiv 1 \bmod p$ a nonabelian group of order $pq$ exists.
  8. (Bonus #8) Solvability of $S_n$.
    Call $G$ solvable when there is a chain $G = G_0 \supseteq G_1 \supseteq \cdots \supseteq G_n = 1$ with each $G_{i+1}$ normal in $G_i$ and $G_i/G_{i+1}$ abelian. The commutator subgroup $[G,G]$ is the subgroup generated by all $ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$, and the derived series is $G^{(0)} = G$, $G^{(i+1)} = [G^{(i)}, G^{(i)}]$. The two notions match. For $N \trianglelefteq H$, the quotient $H/N$ is abelian exactly when $[H,H] \subseteq N$, so induction gives $G^{(i)} \subseteq G_i$ for any such chain. Conversely the derived series is itself such a chain. Hence $G$ is solvable if and only if $G^{(n)} = 1$ for some $n$.

    (a) Exhibit $V_4 \trianglelefteq A_4$ and show $S_4 \supset A_4 \supset V_4 \supset 1$ has abelian quotients.

    (b) Explain where $V_4$ comes from, using the three ways to split $\{1,2,3,4\}$ into two pairs.

    (c) For $n \ge 5$ compute the derived series of $S_n$ and conclude it is not solvable.
  9. (Bonus #9) Automorphism rigidity.
    Here $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$ is the group of isomorphisms $G \to G$ under composition. Suppose $|\mathrm{Aut}(G)| = 1$.

    (a) Show $G$ is abelian.

    (b) Show every element satisfies $x^2 = e$.

    (c) Conclude $G$ is trivial or $\mathbb{Z}/2$.
  10. (Bonus #10) Prime ideals, maximal ideals, and Zorn's lemma.
    Rings are commutative with an identity $1 \ne 0$. An ideal $I \subseteq R$ is an additive subgroup with $rI \subseteq I$ for every $r \in R$. It is proper when $I \ne R$, which happens exactly when $1 \notin I$.

    A proper ideal $P$ is prime when $ab \in P$ forces $a \in P$ or $b \in P$. A proper ideal $M$ is maximal when the only ideal strictly containing it is $R$.

    (a) Prove $P$ is prime if and only if $R/P$ is a domain.

    (b) Prove $M$ is maximal if and only if $R/M$ is a field.

    (c) Conclude every maximal ideal is prime, and give a prime ideal that is not maximal.

    (d) State Zorn's lemma and use it to show every proper ideal is contained in a maximal ideal.
  11. (Bonus #11) The fundamental theorem of finitely generated abelian groups.
    All groups in this item are abelian, written additively. The torsion subgroup $tG$ of an abelian group $G$ is the set of elements of finite order, a subgroup because a difference of elements of finite order has finite order.

    The theorem. Every finitely generated abelian group is isomorphic to \[\mathbb{Z}/d_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb{Z}/d_r \oplus \mathbb{Z}^{s}, \qquad d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \cdots \mid d_r,\ d_i \ge 2,\] and the $d_i$ and $s$ are uniquely determined. Many courses deduce this from the theorem on modules over a PID. This item proves it from scratch, since the special case is where the intuition lives.

    (a) Show every subgroup $K$ of $\mathbb{Z}^n$ is free abelian of rank at most $n$, meaning there are $k_1,\dots,k_m \in K$ with $m \le n$ such that every element of $K$ is a unique integer combination of them.

    (b) Show every abelian group with $n$ generators is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}^n/K$ for a subgroup $K$, and package $K$ as an integer matrix.

    (c) Show every integer matrix can be transformed, by swapping two rows, negating a row, adding an integer multiple of one row to another, and the same three moves on columns, into $\mathrm{diag}(d_1,\dots,d_r,0,\dots,0)$ with $d_1 \mid \cdots \mid d_r$.

    (d) Explain what the operations do to the pair $K \subseteq \mathbb{Z}^n$, and conclude the existence of the decomposition.

    (e) Deduce the elementary divisor form, where the finite part is a direct sum of cyclic groups of prime power order.

    (f) Prove uniqueness of $s$, of the elementary divisors, and hence of the invariant factors $d_i$.
  12. (Bonus #12) The structure theorem, both normal forms, and Smith normal form.
    An $R$-module $M$ is an abelian group with a multiplication $R\times M \to M$ satisfying $r(m+m') = rm+rm'$, $(r+r')m = rm+r'm$, $(rr')m = r(r'm)$, and $1m = m$. Submodules, quotients, and $R$-linear maps (forming $\mathrm{Hom}_R(M,N)$) behave as for vector spaces.

    For $R = \mathbb{Z}$ everything in this item was proved by hand in item 11. Here we record the general PID version and make it computational.

    (a) State the invariant factor and elementary divisor decompositions for a finitely generated module over a PID, a principal ideal domain, meaning a domain in which every ideal is generated by a single element.

    (b) Give the translation in both directions.

    (c) Show how to compute $\mathrm{coker}(A)$ for an integer matrix $A$ by Smith normal form.

    (d) Carry this out for $A = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} 2 & 4 & 4 \\ -6 & 6 & 12 \\ 10 & 4 & 16 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
  13. (Bonus #13) Counting the abelian groups of a given order.
    (a) Use the structure theorem of item 12 to show that the abelian groups of order $n = p_1^{a_1}\cdots p_r^{a_r}$, up to isomorphism, correspond to choices of one partition of each exponent $a_i$. So their number is $p(a_1)\cdots p(a_r)$, where $p(a)$ counts the partitions of $a$, the ways of writing $a$ as an unordered sum of positive integers.

    (b) Count the abelian groups of order 360.

    (c) List them all in elementary divisor form.

    (d) Give the invariant factor form of each.
  14. (Bonus #14) The Chinese remainder theorem.
    Let $I + J = R$.

    (a) Prove $IJ = I \cap J$.

    (b) Prove $R/IJ \cong R/I \times R/J$.

    (c) State the version for $n$ pairwise comaximal ideals.

    (d) Apply it to describe $\mathbb{Z}/n$ and its idempotents, the elements with $e^2 = e$.
  15. (Bonus #15) Gauss's lemma.
    Let $R$ be a UFD with fraction field $F$. A UFD, a unique factorization domain, is a domain in which every nonzero nonunit is a product of irreducibles, uniquely up to reordering and unit factors. Equivalently, factorizations into irreducibles exist and every irreducible element is prime. The content $c(f)$ of $f \in R[x]$ is the gcd of its coefficients. Call $f$ primitive when $c(f)$ is a unit.

    (a) Prove a product of primitive polynomials is primitive.

    (b) Deduce the content formula $c(fg) = c(f)c(g)$ up to units.

    (c) Prove a primitive $f$ of positive degree is irreducible in $R[x]$ if and only if it is irreducible in $F[x]$.

    (d) Deduce $R$ a UFD implies $R[x]$ a UFD.
  16. (Bonus #16) Eisenstein, reduction mod $p$, and the shift.
    (a) Prove the Eisenstein criterion. Let $R$ be a UFD with fraction field $F$, let $f = a_nx^n + \cdots + a_0 \in R[x]$ with $n \ge 1$, and let $p$ be a prime of $R$ with $p \mid a_i$ for all $i < n$, $p \nmid a_n$, and $p^2 \nmid a_0$. Then $f$ admits no factorization $f = gh$ in $R[x]$ with $\deg g, \deg h \ge 1$, and hence is irreducible in $F[x]$.

    (b) Prove the reduction criterion. Suppose $p$ does not divide the leading coefficient $\mathrm{lc}(f)$, and the reduction $\bar f$ is irreducible in $(R/p)[x]$. Then $f$ admits no factorization in $R[x]$ into two polynomials of positive degree, and hence is irreducible in $F[x]$.

    (c) Show $\Phi_p(x) = 1 + x + \cdots + x^{p-1}$ becomes Eisenstein after $x \mapsto x+1$, and conclude it is irreducible.

    (d) Explain why Eisenstein cannot work for $x^4+4$, and confirm by factoring it.
  17. (Bonus #17) Degrees, simple extensions, and inversion.
    A field extension $K/F$ is a field $K$ containing $F$ as a subfield. Its degree $[K:F]$ is $\dim_F K$, and the extension is finite when its degree is. An element of $K$ is algebraic over $F$ when it is a root of some nonzero polynomial with coefficients in $F$, and the extension is algebraic when every element is. A splitting field of a polynomial $f$ over $F$ is an extension in which $f$ factors into linear factors and which is generated over $F$ by the roots of $f$. We take as known that splitting fields exist and are unique up to isomorphism fixing $F$.

    An element $\alpha$ of an extension of $F$ is algebraic over $F$ when it is a root of some nonzero polynomial in $F[x]$. Its minimal polynomial $m_\alpha$ is the monic generator of the kernel of the evaluation map $F[x] \to F[\alpha]$, $x \mapsto \alpha$. We write $F(\alpha)$ for the smallest field containing $F$ and $\alpha$.

    (a) Prove the tower law, that for a tower of fields $F \subseteq K \subseteq L$ with $[L:K]$ and $[K:F]$ finite, $[L:F] = [L:K]\,[K:F]$.

    (b) Prove $F(\alpha)\cong F[x]/(m_\alpha)$ with basis $1,\alpha,\dots,\alpha^{n-1}$.

    (c) Given $g(\alpha) \ne 0$, express $g(\alpha)^{-1}$ as a polynomial in $\alpha$.

    (d) Carry this out for $\theta$ a root of $x^3+9x+6$, computing $(1+\theta)^{-1}$.
  18. (Bonus #18) Separability and the derivative.
    (a) Prove $f$ has a repeated root in a splitting field if and only if $\gcd(f,f')\ne1$.

    (b) Prove fields of characteristic zero are perfect, where a field is perfect when every irreducible polynomial over it is separable, that is, has no repeated root in a splitting field.

    (c) Prove finite fields are perfect.

    (d) Give an inseparable extension, meaning an algebraic extension containing an element whose minimal polynomial has a repeated root.
  19. (Bonus #19) The finite field lattice.
    (a) Prove $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ exists and is unique up to isomorphism.

    (b) Prove $\mathbb{F}_{p^m}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ if and only if $m \mid n$.

    (c) Prove any finite subgroup of $K^\times$ for a field $K$ is cyclic.

    (d) Explain why $(\mathbb{Z}/n)^\times$ need not be cyclic.
  20. (Bonus #20) Coprime degrees.
    Let $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}] = m$ and $[\mathbb{Q}(\beta):\mathbb{Q}] = n$ with $\gcd(m,n)=1$.

    (a) Show $mn$ divides $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha,\beta):\mathbb{Q}]$.

    (b) Show $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha,\beta):\mathbb{Q}] \le mn$.

    (c) Conclude, and show both hypotheses are needed.
  21. (Bonus #21) The Galois correspondence.
    Call a finite extension $K/F$ Galois when $K$ is the splitting field over $F$ of a separable polynomial. Write $\mathrm{Gal}(K/F) = \mathrm{Aut}(K/F)$ for the automorphisms of $K$ fixing $F$ pointwise. For finite extensions four conditions are equivalent, and we use them interchangeably. The first is that $K/F$ is Galois. The second is $|\mathrm{Aut}(K/F)| = [K:F]$. The third is $K^{\mathrm{Aut}(K/F)} = F$. The fourth is that $K/F$ is normal, meaning every irreducible in $F[x]$ with one root in $K$ splits in $K$, and separable, as in item 18. Part (a) below proves the key comparison behind these equivalences.

    Let $K/F$ be finite Galois with group $G$.

    (a) Prove Artin's theorem, that $[K:K^H] = |H|$ for $H \le G$.

    (b) Prove the maps $E\mapsto \mathrm{Gal}(K/E)$ and $H \mapsto K^H$ are mutually inverse and inclusion reversing.

    (c) Prove $E/F$ is normal exactly when $\mathrm{Gal}(K/E)\trianglelefteq G$, with quotient $\mathrm{Gal}(E/F)$.

    (d) Apply to $[K:F] = 36$ to produce intermediate fields of degrees 4 and 9, and a proper nontrivial Galois subextension.
  22. (Bonus #22) Cyclotomic extensions.
    (a) Prove the $n$th cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_n(x) = \prod_{a} (x - \zeta_n^a)$, the product over $1 \le a \le n$ with $\gcd(a,n) = 1$ and $\zeta_n = e^{2\pi i/n}$, is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$. Its degree is $\varphi(n)$, Euler's function, which counts the integers $1 \le a \le n$ coprime to $n$.

    (b) Prove $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/n)^\times$.

    (c) Describe the subfields of $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{17})$ and find the quadratic one.
  23. (Bonus #23) The Galois group of $x^p - a$.
    Let $p$ be an odd prime and $a \in \mathbb{Q}$ not a $p$th power.

    (a) Identify the splitting field and compute its degree.

    (b) Show $G$ embeds in the affine group $\{i \mapsto bi+c\}$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$.

    (c) Show $G$ is the full affine group, of order $p(p-1)$.

    (d) Show $G$ is solvable and nonabelian.
  24. (Bonus #24) Minimal and characteristic polynomials.
    (a) Prove the Cayley--Hamilton theorem, that every square matrix $A$ over a commutative ring satisfies its own characteristic polynomial, $\chi_A(A) = 0$, where $\chi_A(x) = \det(xI - A)$.

    (b) Prove $m_A \mid \chi_A$, where the minimal polynomial $m_A$ is the monic generator of the ideal $\{f \in F[x] : f(A) = 0\}$.

    (c) Prove $m_A$ and $\chi_A$ have the same irreducible factors.

    (d) Prove $A$ is diagonalizable if and only if $m_A$ splits with distinct roots. Diagonalizable means similar to a diagonal matrix, equivalently that $V$ has a basis of eigenvectors.

    (e) Give two matrices with the same minimal polynomial that are not similar.
  25. (Bonus #25) Commuting diagonalizable matrices.
    (a) Show that if $AB = BA$ then $B$ preserves each eigenspace $E_\lambda(A) = \ker(A - \lambda I)$ of $A$.

    (b) Show a restriction of a diagonalizable operator to an invariant subspace is diagonalizable.

    (c) Conclude commuting diagonalizable matrices are simultaneously diagonalizable.
  26. (Bonus #26) Local rings, and three examples.
    Call $R$ local when it has exactly one maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$. For a multiplicatively closed set $S \subseteq R$ with $1 \in S$, the localization $S^{-1}R$ consists of fractions $a/s$ with $a \in R$, $s \in S$, where $a/s = b/t$ when $u(at - bs) = 0$ for some $u \in S$, added and multiplied as usual. Here $R_P$ denotes $S^{-1}R$ for $S = R\setminus P$ with $P$ prime.

    (a) Prove $R$ is local if and only if its nonunits form an ideal.

    (b) Prove that for $P$ prime, $R_P$ is local with maximal ideal $PR_P$.

    (c) Describe which primes survive localization at $P$.

    (d) Apply (a) to show $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)} = \{a/b \in \mathbb{Q} : p \nmid b\}$, the localization of $\mathbb{Z}$ at the prime $(p)$, and the formal power series ring $K[[x]]$ are local, and that $\mathbb{C}[x,y]/(y^2-x^3)$ is not.
  27. (Bonus #27) Prime correspondence under quotients and localization.
    (a) Prove ideals of $R/I$ correspond to ideals of $R$ containing $I$.

    (b) For $S$ multiplicative, show every ideal of $S^{-1}R$ has the form $S^{-1}\mathfrak{b}$.

    (c) Show the correspondence is not injective on all ideals, and identify exactly where it is.

    (d) Prove primes of $S^{-1}R$ correspond bijectively to primes of $R$ disjoint from $S$.

    (e) Find all maximal ideals of $S^{-1}\mathbb{Z}$ where $S$ is the set of powers of 100.
  28. (Bonus #28) The nilradical is the intersection of the primes.
    An element $x$ is nilpotent when $x^n = 0$ for some $n \ge 1$, and the nilradical $\mathrm{nil}(R)$ is the set of nilpotent elements. It is an ideal: if $x^n = y^m = 0$ then every term of the binomial expansion of $(x+y)^{n+m}$ contains $x^n$ or $y^m$, and $(rx)^n = r^nx^n = 0$.

    (a) Show every nilpotent element lies in every prime ideal.

    (b) Let $x$ be non-nilpotent and $S = \{1,x,x^2,\dots\}$. Use Zorn's lemma, which says that every nonempty partially ordered set in which every chain has an upper bound in the set contains a maximal element, to produce an ideal $P$ maximal among those disjoint from $S$.

    (c) Prove that such a $P$ is prime.

    (d) Conclude $\mathrm{nil}(R) = \bigcap_{P \text{ prime}} P$, and say what the statement means geometrically.
  29. (Bonus #29) The two canonical isomorphisms.
    The tensor product $M \otimes_R N$ is the module generated by symbols $m \otimes n$ subject to bilinearity: $(m + m')\otimes n = m\otimes n + m'\otimes n$, $m \otimes (n + n') = m\otimes n + m\otimes n'$, and $rm \otimes n = m \otimes rn = r(m \otimes n)$. Its universal property: $R$-bilinear maps $M \times N \to P$ correspond exactly to $R$-linear maps $M\otimes_R N \to P$, by composing with $(m,n) \mapsto m\otimes n$.

    (a) Prove $M \otimes_R R/I \cong M/IM$.

    (b) Deduce $R/I \otimes_R R/J \cong R/(I+J)$ and $\mathbb{Z}/a \otimes \mathbb{Z}/b \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(a,b)$.

    (c) Prove $\mathrm{Hom}_R(R/I, M) \cong \{m \in M : Im = 0\}$.

    (d) Compute $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n)$.

    (e) Say what tensoring with $R/I$ is doing conceptually, and use it to explain $\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} = 0$.
  30. (Bonus #30) Exactness properties of tensor and localization.
    A sequence $A \xrightarrow{f} B \xrightarrow{g} C$ is exact at $B$ when $\mathrm{im}\,f = \ker g$. A short exact sequence $0 \to A \to B \to C \to 0$ is exact at $A$, $B$, and $C$. A module $N$ is flat when $-\otimes_R N$ preserves injections. Equivalently it carries short exact sequences to short exact sequences, the rest being automatic by (a).

    (a) Prove $-\otimes_R N$ is right exact. If $A \xrightarrow{f} B \xrightarrow{g} C \to 0$ is exact, then so is $A\otimes_R N \to B\otimes_R N \to C\otimes_R N \to 0$.

    (b) Give an injection whose tensor is not injective.

    (c) Prove $S^{-1}M \cong S^{-1}R\otimes_R M$.

    (d) Prove $S^{-1}(-)$ is exact, and conclude $S^{-1}R$ is flat.
  31. (Bonus #31) The splitting lemma and projectivity.
    Let $0 \to A \xrightarrow{f} B \xrightarrow{g} C \to 0$ be exact.

    (a) Prove the equivalence of a retraction $r$ with $rf = \mathrm{id}_A$, a section $s$ with $gs = \mathrm{id}_C$, and an isomorphism $B \cong A\oplus C$ compatible with the maps.

    (b) Call $P$ projective when it has the lifting property, that for every surjection $\rho : B \to C$ and every $h : P \to C$ there is a lift $\tilde h : P \to B$ with $\rho\tilde h = h$, or equivalently, $\mathrm{Hom}_R(P,-)$ preserves surjections. Prove this is equivalent to the condition that every surjection onto $P$ splits, and to the condition that $P$ is a direct summand of a free module.

    (c) Use (b) to explain why $0\to\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{2}\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/2\to0$ does not split, while every short exact sequence of vector spaces does.
  32. (Bonus #32) Baer's criterion and injectivity over a PID.
    (a) Prove Baer's criterion. An $R$-module $Q$ is injective, meaning every homomorphism $A \to Q$ extends along every inclusion $A \subseteq B$ of $R$-modules, if and only if every homomorphism $I \to Q$ from an ideal $I \subseteq R$ extends to $R$.

    (b) Prove that over a PID injective is equivalent to divisible, where $Q$ is divisible when $aQ = Q$ for every nonzero $a \in R$.

    (c) Deduce $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ are injective $\mathbb{Z}$-modules and $\mathbb{Z}$ is not.
  33. (Bonus #33) Flat, torsion free, and free over a PID.
    (a) Prove flat implies torsion free over any domain, where $M$ is torsion free when $am = 0$ with $a \in R$ nonzero and $m \in M$ forces $m = 0$.

    (b) Prove a finitely generated torsion free module over a PID is free.

    (c) Prove torsion free implies flat over a PID.

    (d) Show $\mathbb{Q}$ is flat and not projective over $\mathbb{Z}$.

    (e) Show $(x,y) \subset k[x,y]$ is torsion free and not free, and say why (b) cannot be repeated.

  36. (Bonus #36) Krull dimension.
    The Krull dimension of a ring $R$ is the largest $n$ for which there is a chain \[P_0 \subsetneq P_1 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq P_n\] of prime ideals of $R$. We count the links, not the primes, so a chain of two primes has length 1. This word is new here, so the point of this item is to make it concrete.

    (a) Show a field has Krull dimension 0, and that a domain of Krull dimension 0 is a field.

    (b) Show a PID that is not a field has Krull dimension exactly 1. In particular $\mathbb{Z}$ and $k[x]$ have dimension 1.

    (c) Exhibit chains showing $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ and $k[x,y]$ have Krull dimension at least 2, verifying that every ideal used is prime.

    (d) Explain the geometric meaning.

    _p$ and Frobenius orbits} Let $p \nmid n$. This item explains how $x^n - 1$, and with it $\Phi_n$, factors over $\mathbb{F}_p$, and why the answer is pure combinatorics. (a) Show the $n$th roots of unity $\mu_n \subseteq \overline{\mathbb{F}_p}$ form a cyclic group of order $n$, and that the Frobenius $\varphi(a) = a^p$ maps $\mu_n$ to itself. (c) Deduce the shape of $\Phi_n$ mod $p$. It factors into $\varphi(n)/d$ distinct irreducibles, all of the same degree $d$, where $d$ is the multiplicative order of $p$ in $(\mathbb{Z}/n)^\times$. (d) Give the general method for making the factorization explicit, and carry it out for $\Phi_8 = x^4+1$ over $\mathbb{F}_7$.

  49. (Bonus #49) Free groups and rank.
    The free group $F(S)$ on a set $S$ consists of the reduced words in $S \cup S^{-1}$. Equivalently, every function from $S$ into a group $G$ extends uniquely to a homomorphism $F(S) \to G$. A free basis of a group is a subset with this property, and the rank is its size. We write $F_n$ for the free group of rank $n$.

    (a) Let $F_2 = \langle a,b\rangle$ and $\phi: F_2 \to \mathbb{Z}$ send $a \mapsto 0$, $b\mapsto 1$. Show $\ker\phi$ is generated by the conjugates $a_i = b^iab^{-i}$, $i \in \mathbb{Z}$, and that they form a free basis.

    (c) Show by contrast that a subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}^n$ has rank at most $n$.

    (d) Explain why abelianizing destroys the phenomenon in (b).
  50. (Bonus #50) Prime avoidance.
    The statement is that if an ideal $I$ satisfies $I \subseteq P_1 \cup \cdots \cup P_n$, where the $P_i$ are ideals of which all but at most two are prime, then $I \subseteq P_i$ for some $i$. Nothing beyond induction and one trick is needed.

    (b) Prove the general case by induction on $n$.

    (c) Show primeness cannot simply be dropped once $n = 3$. In $R = \mathbb{F}_2[x,y]/(x^2,xy,y^2)$, the ideal $\mathfrak{m} = (x,y)$ is the union of the three ideals $(x)$, $(y)$, $(x+y)$ but is contained in none of them.
  51. (Bonus #51) Finite nilpotent groups and the Sylow decomposition.

    (a) Show every finite $p$-group is nilpotent.

    (b) Show that in a nilpotent group every proper subgroup is a proper subgroup of its own normalizer.

    (c) Show that for a Sylow $p$-subgroup $P$ of any finite group, $N_G(N_G(P)) = N_G(P)$. Conclude that in a finite nilpotent group every Sylow subgroup is normal.

    (d) Show that if every Sylow subgroup of a finite group $G$ is normal, then $G$ is isomorphic to the direct product of its Sylow subgroups. Conclude that a finite group is nilpotent if and only if it is the direct product of its Sylow subgroups.

  52. (Bonus #52) The determinant trick and Nakayama's lemma.

    (b) Deduce Nakayama's lemma. If $(R,\mathfrak{m})$ is local and $\mathfrak{m}M = M$, then $M = 0$.

    (c) Deduce the generating criterion. For $(R,\mathfrak{m})$ local with residue field $k = R/\mathfrak{m}$, elements $m_1,\dots,m_n \in M$ generate $M$ if and only if their images span the $k$-vector space $M/\mathfrak{m}M$.

    (d) Show the images of a minimal generating set form a $k$-basis of $M/\mathfrak{m}M$. In particular every minimal generating set has exactly $\dim_k M/\mathfrak{m}M$ elements, and a vanishing $R$-linear combination of a minimal generating set has all its coefficients in $\mathfrak{m}$, so it reduces to the trivial linear combination over $k$.
  53. (Bonus #53) The Jacobson radical and the unit criterion.

    (b) Compute $J(R)$ for a local ring, for $\mathbb{Z}$, for $\mathbb{Z}/12$, and for $K[[x]]$.

    /n$} This is the structure behind the standard problems asking which modules over $\mathbb{Z}/n$ are projective or injective. Work with finitely generated modules throughout. (a) Show $\mathbb{Z}/n \cong \prod_i \mathbb{Z}/p_i^{a_i}$ and that a module over a finite product of rings is exactly a tuple of modules over the factors. (b) Let $R = \mathbb{Z}/p^e$. (c) Show such a module is projective if and only if every $c_j = e$, that is if and only if it is free. (d) Using Baer's criterion, show it is injective if and only if every $c_j = e$. (e) Show it is flat if and only if every $c_j = e$. (f) Conclude that over $\mathbb{Z}/n$ the three notions coincide, and describe the common class concretely.   55. (Bonus #55) Flat, finitely presented, and locally free.
    Recall from item 30 that $M$ is flat when $-\otimes_R M$ preserves injections, and from item 31(b) that $M$ is projective exactly when it is a direct summand of a free module. A presentation of $M$ is an exact sequence \[R^{\,n} \xrightarrow{\;A\;} R^{\,m} \xrightarrow{\;\pi\;} M \to 0,\] realizing $M$ as the cokernel of the $m\times n$ matrix $A$. The images $\pi(e_1),\dots,\pi(e_m)$ generate $M$, and the columns of $A$ span the relations among them. $M$ is finitely presented when it admits such a presentation with $m$ and $n$ finite. Finally $M$ is locally free of finite rank when $M_{\mathfrak{m}}$ is a finitely generated free $R_{\mathfrak{m}}$-module for every maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$, with localization as in items 26 and 30(c).

    This item proves that for a finitely presented module the conditions flat, projective, and locally free of finite rank are equivalent, and that over a local ring each is equivalent to free. Everything below runs on the principle, made precise in (b), that flatness means $M$ satisfies no relations beyond those already forced over $R$. We take one localization fact as given. For $M$ finitely presented and $\mathfrak{m}$ maximal, the natural map \[\mathrm{Hom}_R(M,N)_{\mathfrak{m}} \to \mathrm{Hom}_{R_{\mathfrak{m}}}(M_{\mathfrak{m}},N_{\mathfrak{m}}), \qquad f/s \mapsto \bigl(x/t \mapsto f(x)/st\bigr),\] is an isomorphism, natural in $N$. (Applying $\mathrm{Hom}_R(-,N)$ to a presentation writes $\mathrm{Hom}_R(M,N)$ as the kernel of a map $N^m \to N^n$. Localization is exact by item 30(d), and the localized presentation presents $M_{\mathfrak{m}}$, so both sides are the kernel of the same map over $R_{\mathfrak{m}}$. Finiteness of both $m$ and $n$ is what makes this work.)

    (a) Let $A$ present $M$ as above, let $\mathfrak{m}$ be maximal with residue field $k = R/\mathfrak{m}$, and let $\bar A$ be $A$ with entries reduced mod $\mathfrak{m}$. Show $M \otimes_R k \cong \mathrm{coker}(\bar A)$, so that the fiber dimension $\dim_k M \otimes_R k$ equals $m - \mathrm{rank}\,\bar A$. Conclude that a finitely generated free module has the same fiber dimension at every $\mathfrak{m}$. Then compute all the fiber dimensions of $\mathbb{Z}/6$ over $\mathbb{Z}$, together with $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} (\mathbb{Z}/6)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}$, and note the jump.

    (b) Prove the relations criterion. If $M$ is flat, $a \in R^{1\times n}$ is a row over $R$, and $x \in M^n$ is a column of module elements with $ax = \sum_i a_i x_i = 0$, then there are a matrix $B \in R^{\,n\times s}$ and a column $y \in M^s$ with \[x = By \qquad\text{and}\qquad aB = 0.\] So $a$ kills $x$ only because $a$ already kills, over $R$ itself, a matrix through which $x$ factors. The converse is also true but is not needed below. Then show that the relation $2\cdot\bar 1 = 0$ in $\mathbb{Z}/2$ admits no such $B$ and $y$, recovering the fact that $\mathbb{Z}/2$ is not flat over $\mathbb{Z}$.

    (c) Let $(R,\mathfrak{m})$ be local and let $M$ satisfy the conclusion of (b), as any flat module does. Show that $ax = 0$ with $a \in R$ and $x \in M \setminus \mathfrak{m}M$ forces $a = 0$.

    (d) Keep the hypotheses of (c), and let $x_1,\dots,x_n \in M$ have linearly independent images in the $k$-vector space $M/\mathfrak{m}M$. Prove by induction on $n$ that $x_1,\dots,x_n$ are linearly independent over $R$. Conclude with item 52(c) that a finitely generated flat module over a local ring is free. Finite presentation is not needed here.

    (e) Let $M$ be flat over $R$ and $\mathfrak{m}$ maximal. By clearing denominators, show that $M_{\mathfrak{m}}$ satisfies the conclusion of (b) as a module over $R_{\mathfrak{m}}$. Conclude that a finitely generated flat module over any ring is locally free of finite rank.

    (f) Show free modules are flat, and that a direct summand of a flat module is flat. Conclude that projective implies flat.

    (g) Show $N = 0$ if and only if $N_{\mathfrak{m}} = 0$ for every maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$.

    (h) Let $M$ be finitely presented and locally free of finite rank, and let $\pi : R^m \to M$ be the surjection of a presentation. Using the given isomorphism, (g), and item 31(b), show that composition with $\pi$ maps $\mathrm{Hom}_R(M,R^m)$ onto $\mathrm{Hom}_R(M,M)$. Conclude that $\pi$ splits, that $M$ is projective, and assemble the whole theorem.

    (i) Show that if $M$ is finitely presented and $M_{\mathfrak{m}} \cong R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\,r}$, then over $R_{\mathfrak{m}}$ there are invertible matrices $U$ and $V$ with \[UAV = \begin{bmatrix} I_{m-r} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix},\] so the presentation matrix localizes to a projection, and the fiber dimension at $\mathfrak{m}$ is exactly $r$. Deduce that over a domain a finitely generated flat module has the same fiber dimension at every maximal ideal, so the jump in (a) already shows $\mathbb{Z}/6$ is not flat. Finally show both hypotheses are needed. $\mathbb{Z}/n$ is finitely presented and not flat, while $\mathbb{Q}$ is flat over $\mathbb{Z}$ but $\mathbb{Q}_{(p)} = \mathbb{Q}$ is not free over $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$, so flat does not imply locally free without finite generation.
