Galois groups of specific polynomials

Def. Discriminant of a polynomial. (D&F §14.6) For monic $f \in F[x]$ with roots $\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n$ in a splitting field, the discriminant is $\Delta(f) = \prod_{i < j} (\alpha_i - \alpha_j)^2$. Discriminant lives in $F$ (it is a symmetric polynomial in the roots, so $F$-coefficients are preserved). $\Delta \neq 0$ iff all roots are distinct, i.e., $f$ is separable. The square root $\sqrt{\Delta}$ is fixed by $\mathrm{Gal}(f)$ iff the Galois group is contained in $A_n$ (the alternating group). So $\sqrt{\Delta} \in F$ detects whether $\mathrm{Gal}(f) \subseteq A_n$.
Prop. Cubic Galois group via discriminant. (D&F §14.6) For $f = x^3 + px + q$ irreducible over $F$ with $\mathrm{char}(F) \neq 2,3$, the discriminant is $\Delta(f) = -4p^3 - 27q^2$. Two cases: $\sqrt{\Delta} \in F$ or $\sqrt{\Delta} \notin F$. If $\sqrt{\Delta} \in F$, then $\mathrm{Gal}(f) \cong \mathbb{Z}/3$ (cyclic, $f$ splits over the unique cubic extension). Otherwise $\mathrm{Gal}(f) \cong S_3$ (nonabelian, the generic case).
Computation. Galois group of $x^3 - 3x + 1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. First check irreducibility: rational root test on $x^3 - 3x + 1$. Candidates $\pm 1$ give $1 - 3 + 1 = -1 \neq 0$ and $-1 + 3 + 1 = 3 \neq 0$, so irreducible. Write $f = x^3 - 3x + 1$ in form $x^3 + px + q$ where $p = -3, q = 1$. Discriminant: $\Delta = -4(-3)^3 - 27(1)^2 = -4(-27) - 27 = 108 - 27 = 81 = 9^2$. Since $\sqrt{\Delta} = 9 \in \mathbb{Q}$, the Galois group is $\mathrm{Gal}(f) \cong \mathbb{Z}/3$.
Prop. Galois group of $x^n - a$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. (D&F §14.7) Assume $x^n - a$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ with $n \geq 3$. Let $\alpha = a^{1/n}$ and $\zeta_n$ a primitive $n$-th root. The splitting field is $K = \mathbb{Q}(\alpha, \zeta_n)$ of degree $[K:\mathbb{Q}] = n \cdot \varphi(n)$ (product of the two extension degrees, which are coprime). The Galois group $G = \mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ is a subgroup of the semidirect product $\mathbb{Z}/n \rtimes (\mathbb{Z}/n)^\times$. The group is non-abelian when $n \geq 3$ because the conjugation action is nontrivial.
Computation. Galois group of $x^5 - 2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. Irreducibility: Eisenstein criterion at $p=2$ (leading coeff $1$ coprime to $2$, but $2 \mid 2$, $2^2 \nmid 2$). Splitting field: $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[5]{2}, \zeta_5)$. Degree: $[K:\mathbb{Q}] = 5 \cdot \varphi(5) = 5 \cdot 4 = 20$. Galois group: $\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ is isomorphic to the non-abelian semidirect product $\mathbb{Z}/5 \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/4$, order $20$. It is non-abelian because $\mathbb{Z}/4$ acts nontrivially on $\mathbb{Z}/5$ by the action $g \cdot a \equiv a \cdot j^g \pmod{5}$ for some $j$.
Prop. Cyclotomic and Kronecker-Weber theorem. (D&F §13.6, §14.5) $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^\times$, always abelian (since units form an abelian group). Kronecker-Weber: Every finite abelian extension $K/\mathbb{Q}$ is contained in some cyclotomic field $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$. So cyclotomic fields exhaust all finite abelian Galois extensions of $\mathbb{Q}$.
Thm. Every finite group is a Galois group over function fields. (D&F §14.6, Ex. 46) For any finite group $G$, there exists a Galois extension $L/K$ of fields with $\mathrm{Gal}(L/K) \cong G$. Construction: embed $G \hookrightarrow S_n$ as a transitive subgroup. Take $L = \mathbb{Q}(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$ (purely transcendental), and let $G$ act by permuting variables. Set $K = L^G$ (fixed field). Then $L/K$ is Galois with group $G$. Note: $K$ is not usually $\mathbb{Q}$. The inverse Galois problem over $\mathbb{Q}$ (whether all finite groups realize over $\mathbb{Q}$) remains open.
Thm (Galois-Abel-Ruffini). (D&F §14.7) A polynomial $f \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$ is solvable by radicals (i.e., roots expressible in $+, -, \times, \div, \sqrt[n]{\ }$) iff its Galois group $\mathrm{Gal}(f)$ is a solvable group. Solvable means: a composition series where all quotients have prime order. For $n \geq 5$, the symmetric group $S_n$ is not solvable. Therefore a generic degree-$n$ polynomial with $n \geq 5$ has Galois group $S_n$, hence is not solvable by radicals. This settles classical problems on quintic and higher-degree equations.
Fun fact. Computing Galois groups is an art. For small cases (cubics, quartics, $x^n - a$), discriminant or direct symmetry arguments work. For higher degrees, use Frobenius splitting in primes, Newton polygon factorizations, or reduction modulo $p$ to guess the Galois group, then verify. The problem is hard: no uniform algorithm is known.
