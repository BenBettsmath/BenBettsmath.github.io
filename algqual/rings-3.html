Localization

Def. Localization. (D&F §7.5) Let $S \subseteq R$ be multiplicatively closed, containing $1$. Then $S^{-1}R$ is the set of pairs $(r, s)$, $s \in S$, written $r/s$, under $r/s \sim r'/s' \iff t(rs' - r's) = 0$ for some $t \in S$. We formally invert all of $S$: addition and multiplication mimic fractions, and $R \to S^{-1}R$ sends $r \mapsto r/1$. When there are zero divisors in $R$, the "for some $t \in S$" is essential for transitivity.
Example. For $R = \mathbb{Z}$, $S = \mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\}$ gives $S^{-1}R = \mathbb{Q}$. $S = \{1, p, p^2, \ldots\}$ gives $\mathbb{Z}[1/p]$ (rationals with only $p$ in the denominator). $S = \mathbb{Z} \setminus (p)$ gives the local ring $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$, where only primes $\neq p$ are invertible.
Non-example / caution. If $0 \in S$ then $S^{-1}R = 0$. The "for some $t \in S$" in the equivalence relation is essential when $R$ has zero divisors: without it, the relation isn't transitive.
Fun fact. Localization kills bad elements. If $r \in S$ then $r$ becomes a unit in $S^{-1}R$. If $t \in R$ would cause problems (like zero divisors), we can remove it from our "good set" and localize at the complement to make it vanish.
Rules. Behavior of ideals under localization.
  • $S^{-1}(I + J) = S^{-1}I + S^{-1}J$, $S^{-1}(I \cap J) = S^{-1}I \cap S^{-1}J$, $S^{-1}(IJ) = (S^{-1}I)(S^{-1}J)$.
  • Primes of $S^{-1}R$ are exactly $S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}$ for primes $\mathfrak{p}$ of $R$ with $\mathfrak{p} \cap S = \emptyset$.
  • Localizing at the complement of a prime $\mathfrak{p}$ produces a local ring $R_\mathfrak{p}$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{p} R_\mathfrak{p}$.
Prop. Universal property of localization. (D&F §15.4, Prop. 36) The map $\iota \colon R \to S^{-1}R$, $r \mapsto r/1$, sends each $s \in S$ to a unit, and is universal for this: any $\phi \colon R \to T$ with $\phi(S) \subseteq T^\times$ factors uniquely as $\bar\phi \colon S^{-1}R \to T$ with $\bar\phi \circ \iota = \phi$, namely $\bar\phi(r/s) = \phi(r) \phi(s)^{-1}$.
Prop. (D&F §7.5, Ex. 3) For $S = \{1, f, f^2, \ldots\}$ with $f$ a nonzerodivisor, $S^{-1}R \cong R[x]/(fx - 1)$ via $r/f^n \mapsto r x^n$. Formally inverting powers of $f$ is equivalent to adjoining a polynomial variable $x$ and imposing $fx = 1$. (By universal property: $R[x] \to S^{-1}R$, $x \mapsto 1/f$, kills $fx - 1$ and factors through.)
Computation. Primes of $\mathbb{Z}[1/6] = S^{-1}\mathbb{Z}$, $S = \{6^n\}$. Primes of $\mathbb{Z}$ are $(0)$ and $(p)$; drop those meeting $S$ (containing $2$ or $3$). So the primes of $\mathbb{Z}[1/6]$ are $(0)$ and $(p)$ for $p \neq 2, 3$, the maximal ones being the $(p)$ with $p \neq 2, 3$.
