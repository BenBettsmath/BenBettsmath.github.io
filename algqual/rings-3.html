Localization

Def. Localization. (D&F §7.5) Let $R$ be a commutative ring with $1$ and let $S \subseteq R$ be multiplicatively closed, containing $1$. Then $S^{-1}R$ is the set of pairs $(r, s)$ with $s \in S$, written $r/s$, modulo the relation $r/s \sim r'/s' \iff t(rs' - r's) = 0$ for some $t \in S$. This makes every element of $S$ invertible in $S^{-1}R$. Addition and multiplication work the same way as for fractions, and $R \to S^{-1}R$ sends $r \mapsto r/1$. We need the extra $t$ when $R$ has zero divisors, since without it the relation isn't transitive.
Example. For $R = \mathbb{Z}$, $S = \mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\}$ gives $S^{-1}R = \mathbb{Q}$. $S = \{1, p, p^2, \ldots\}$ gives $\mathbb{Z}[1/p]$ (rationals whose denominator is a power of $p$). $S = \mathbb{Z} \setminus (p)$ gives the local ring $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$, where only primes $\neq p$ are invertible.
Non-example / caution. If $0 \in S$ then $S^{-1}R = 0$, since taking $t = 0$ makes every $r/s$ equal to $0/1$. The "for some $t \in S$" in the equivalence relation is needed when $R$ has zero divisors. Without it, the relation isn't transitive.
Remark. What localization does to elements. If $s \in S$ then $s$ becomes a unit in $S^{-1}R$, with inverse $1/s$. The cost is that every element of $R$ killed by an element of $S$ becomes $0$ in $S^{-1}R$, since $sr = 0$ with $s \in S$ means $r/1 = 0/1$. So localizing at a set containing zero divisors sends some nonzero elements to $0$.
Rules. Behavior of ideals under localization.
  • For all ideals $I$ and $J$ of $R$ we have $S^{-1}(I + J) = S^{-1}I + S^{-1}J$, $S^{-1}(I \cap J) = S^{-1}I \cap S^{-1}J$, $S^{-1}(IJ) = (S^{-1}I)(S^{-1}J)$.
  • The primes of $S^{-1}R$ are exactly $S^{-1}\mathfrak{p}$ for primes $\mathfrak{p}$ of $R$ with $\mathfrak{p} \cap S = \emptyset$. The primes that meet $S$ disappear, since after localizing they contain a unit.
  • Localizing at the complement of a prime $\mathfrak{p}$ produces a local ring $R_\mathfrak{p}$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{p} R_\mathfrak{p}$.
Prop. Universal property of localization. (D&F §15.4, Prop. 36) The map $\iota \colon R \to S^{-1}R$, $r \mapsto r/1$, sends each $s \in S$ to a unit, and is universal with this property: every ring homomorphism $\phi \colon R \to T$ with $\phi(S) \subseteq T^\times$ factors uniquely through $\iota$, as a homomorphism $\bar\phi \colon S^{-1}R \to T$ with $\bar\phi \circ \iota = \phi$, namely $\bar\phi(r/s) = \phi(r) \phi(s)^{-1}$.
Prop. (D&F §7.5, Ex. 3) Let $f \in R$ be a nonzerodivisor on $R$ and let $S = \{1, f, f^2, \ldots\}$. Then $S^{-1}R \cong R[x]/(fx - 1)$ via $r/f^n \mapsto r x^n$. So inverting $f$ is the same as adding a new variable $x$ and forcing $fx = 1$, that is $x = 1/f$. (To see this, the map $R[x] \to S^{-1}R$, $x \mapsto 1/f$, kills $fx - 1$ so it factors through the quotient, and since $f$ is a unit in $R[x]/(fx - 1)$ with inverse $x$, the universal property of localization gives the map in the other direction.)
Computation. Primes of $\mathbb{Z}[1/6] = S^{-1}\mathbb{Z}$, $S = \{6^n\}$. The primes of $\mathbb{Z}$ are $(0)$ and $(p)$ for prime numbers $p$. Drop the ones that meet $S$, which are the ones containing $2$ or $3$. So the primes of $\mathbb{Z}[1/6]$ are $(0)$ and $(p)$ for $p \neq 2, 3$, the maximal ones being the $(p)$ with $p \neq 2, 3$.
