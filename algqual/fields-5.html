Galois theory

Def. Galois extension. (D&F §14.1) A finite extension $K/F$ is Galois if $|\mathrm{Aut}(K/F)| = [K:F]$, where $\mathrm{Aut}(K/F)$ is the group of automorphisms of $K$ that fix every element of $F$. Every finite extension satisfies $|\mathrm{Aut}(K/F)| \leq [K:F]$, so Galois means there are as many automorphisms as possible. Equivalent conditions: $K/F$ is normal and separable, or $K$ is the splitting field of a separable polynomial over $F$. Write $\mathrm{Gal}(K/F) := \mathrm{Aut}(K/F)$ for the Galois group. In characteristic $0$, all finite extensions are separable, so every finite normal extension is Galois.
Example. $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})/\mathbb{Q}$ is Galois. It is normal (the splitting field of $x^2 - 2$) and separable (characteristic $0$). Its Galois group is $\mathrm{Gal} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$, generated by the non-trivial automorphism $\sqrt{2} \mapsto -\sqrt{2}$. $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)/\mathbb{Q}$ is Galois for all $n \geq 1$.
Non-example. $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})/\mathbb{Q}$ is separable but not normal. It is contained in $\mathbb{R}$, so the other roots $\omega \sqrt[3]{2}, \omega^2 \sqrt[3]{2}$ (where $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$) are not in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})$. Its Galois closure (the smallest extension of it that is Galois over $\mathbb{Q}$) is $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}, \zeta_3)$ with $\zeta_3$ a primitive cube root of unity, of degree $6 = 3 \cdot 2$.
Prop. (D&F §14.1, Prop. 2) If $\sigma \in \mathrm{Aut}(K/F)$ and $\alpha \in K$ is algebraic over $F$, then $\sigma(\alpha)$ is another root of $m_{\alpha,F}$ (the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $F$). So an automorphism can only send a root to another root of the same minimal polynomial, which is what makes it possible to compute Galois groups. Proof: apply $\sigma$ to the relation $m_{\alpha,F}(\alpha) = 0$. Since $m_{\alpha,F} \in F[x]$ and $\sigma$ fixes $F$, the coefficients are fixed, so $m_{\alpha,F}(\sigma(\alpha)) = 0$.
Thm (Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory). (D&F §14.2, Thm. 14) Let $K/F$ be Galois with $G = \mathrm{Gal}(K/F)$. Define: $$E \mapsto \mathrm{Gal}(K/E) \quad \text{and} \quad H \mapsto K^H \text{ (fixed field)}$$ These are inverse bijections between intermediate fields $F \subseteq E \subseteq K$ and subgroups $H \leq G$. So intermediate fields and subgroups correspond, with larger fields corresponding to smaller subgroups. Let $E$ be an intermediate field and $H = \mathrm{Gal}(K/E)$ the corresponding subgroup. Then:
  1. $[K : E] = |H|$ and $[E : F] = [G : H]$ (degrees match orders).
  2. $K/E$ is always Galois with $\mathrm{Gal}(K/E) = H$.
  3. $E/F$ is Galois iff $H$ is normal in $G$. Then $\mathrm{Gal}(E/F) \cong G/H$. (This is because $\mathrm{Gal}(K/\sigma E) = \sigma H \sigma^{-1}$ for every $\sigma \in G$, so the normal subgroups are the ones whose fixed field every $\sigma$ sends to itself.)
  4. Composita: if $E_1, E_2$ are intermediate fields with corresponding subgroups $H_1, H_2$, then $E_1 \cap E_2 \leftrightarrow \langle H_1, H_2 \rangle$ and $E_1 E_2 \leftrightarrow H_1 \cap H_2$ (the correspondence reverses inclusions).
Computation. Galois correspondence for $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3})/\mathbb{Q}$. We have $G \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}/2$ with generators $\sigma: \sqrt{2} \mapsto -\sqrt{2}, \tau: \sqrt{3} \mapsto -\sqrt{3}$. The five subgroups are $\{1\}$ (trivial), $\langle\sigma\rangle, \langle\tau\rangle, \langle\sigma\tau\rangle$, and $G$ itself. Their fixed fields are $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3}), \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3}), \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}), \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{6}), \mathbb{Q}$ respectively (inclusions are reversed).
Computation. Galois group of $x^3 - 2$. The splitting field is $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}, \zeta_3)$, with $[K:\mathbb{Q}] = 6 = 3 \cdot 2$. The Galois group is $G = \mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_3$ (the symmetric group on the 3 roots). The unique normal subgroup of order $3$ is $A_3$, generated by the $3$-cycle $\rho$ that cyclically permutes the three cube roots. Its fixed field is $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)$, degree $2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. This is the only intermediate field (other than $\mathbb{Q}$ and $K$) that is Galois over $\mathbb{Q}$.
Note. Finding a Galois subfield. (D&F §14.2) Given $K/F$ Galois with $G = \mathrm{Gal}(K/F)$, to find an intermediate $E$ that is also Galois over $F$, find a proper normal subgroup $H \trianglelefteq G$ (with $1 \subsetneq H \subsetneq G$), then take $E = K^H$. Ways to find such $H$:
  • $p$-group: If $|G| = p^k$, the center $Z(G)$ is nontrivial. Any subgroup of $Z(G)$ is normal in $G$.
  • Solvable: If $G$ is solvable and not abelian, the commutator subgroup $[G,G]$ is a proper normal subgroup of $G$.
  • Sylow: If a Sylow $p$-subgroup is unique (that is, $n_p = 1$), it is normal in $G$.
  • Non-simple: Any proper nontrivial normal subgroup of $G$ works (use Sylow's theorem or the lower central series to find one).
