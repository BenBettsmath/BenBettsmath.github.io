Tensor products

Def. Tensor product. (D&F §10.4) For a right $R$-module $M$ and left $R$-module $N$, the tensor product $M \otimes_R N$ is the abelian group generated by the symbols $m \otimes n$ for $m \in M$ and $n \in N$, modulo bilinearity and the balanced relation $mr \otimes n = m \otimes rn$ for every $r \in R$. The group $M \otimes_R N$ is the most general place to send pairs $(m, n)$ bilinearly, and the balanced relation lets scalars move from one side of the $\otimes$ to the other. Universal property: for every abelian group $A$, the $R$-balanced maps $M \times N \to A$ correspond bijectively to the group homomorphisms $M \otimes_R N \to A$.
Example. $\mathbb{Q} \otimes_\mathbb{Z} \mathbb{Q} \cong \mathbb{Q}$, since moving integers across the $\otimes$ rewrites any $x \otimes \tfrac{c}{d}$ as $\tfrac{x}{d} \otimes c = \tfrac{cx}{d} \otimes 1$. $\mathbb{R} \otimes_\mathbb{Q} \mathbb{R}$ is an $\mathbb{R}$-vector space of uncountable dimension.
Non-example (vanishing). $\mathbb{Z}/2 \otimes_\mathbb{Z} \mathbb{Z}/3 = 0$, since it's generated by $\bar 1 \otimes \bar 1$, and $\bar 1 \otimes \bar 1 = 3 (\bar 1 \otimes \bar 1) - 2 (\bar 1 \otimes \bar 1) = \bar 1 \otimes \bar 3 - \bar 2 \otimes \bar 1 = 0$.
Rules. Tensor identities (over commutative $R$). (D&F §10.4)
  • $M \otimes_R R \cong M$.
  • $(M \oplus M') \otimes N \cong (M \otimes N) \oplus (M' \otimes N)$ (distributivity over $\oplus$).
  • $M \otimes N \cong N \otimes M$ (commutativity).
  • $(M \otimes N) \otimes P \cong M \otimes (N \otimes P)$ (associativity).
  • $R/I \otimes_R R/J \cong R/(I + J)$ for ideals $I, J$ of $R$.
  • $\mathbb{Z}/m \otimes_\mathbb{Z} \mathbb{Z}/n \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m, n)$ for positive integers $m, n$.
  • $M \otimes_R N$ inherits scalar multiplication, so for a ring $S \supseteq R$, $S \otimes_R M$ is naturally an $S$-module ("base change").
Computation. $\mathbb{Z}/3 \otimes_\mathbb{Z} \mathbb{Z}/2 \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(3,2) = \mathbb{Z}/1 = 0$. $\mathbb{Z}/4 \otimes_\mathbb{Z} \mathbb{Z}/2 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$.
Prop. (D&F §10.5, Ex. 8) Let $I$ be an ideal of a commutative ring $R$ and $M$ an $R$-module. Then $\mathrm{Hom}_R(R/I, M) \cong \{m \in M : Im = 0\}$, the elements of $M$ killed by $I$. An $R$-linear $\phi\colon R/I \to M$ is determined by $\phi(\bar 1)$, and for $\phi$ to be well defined, every element of $I$ must kill $\phi(\bar 1)$. So $\mathrm{Hom}_\mathbb{Z}(\mathbb{Z}/n, M) \cong M[n] = \{m : nm = 0\}$.
Prop (Adjoint associativity). (D&F §10.5, Thm. 43) Let $M, N, P$ be modules over a commutative ring $R$. Then $\mathrm{Hom}_R(M \otimes_R N, P) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_R(M, \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, P))$. This is currying. A bilinear map out of $M \times N$ is the same thing as a map from $M$ to linear maps out of $N$.
Def. Symmetric and exterior powers. (D&F §11.5) Let $V$ be a vector space over a field $F$. Then $S^2 V = (V \otimes V)/\langle v \otimes w - w \otimes v\rangle$ is the symmetric square and $\Lambda^2 V = (V \otimes V)/\langle v \otimes v\rangle$ is the alternating (exterior) square, where the angle brackets denote the subspace spanned by the given elements for all $v, w \in V$. If $\dim V = n$ then $\dim S^2 V = \binom{n+1}{2}$ and $\dim \Lambda^2 V = \binom{n}{2}$, so the dimensions sum to $n^2 = \dim V \otimes V$.
Prop. $V \otimes V \cong S^2 V \oplus \Lambda^2 V$ when $\mathrm{char}(F) \neq 2$. (D&F §11.5) The swap $\sigma(v \otimes w) = w \otimes v$ is an involution, $\sigma^2 = 1$, so its minimal polynomial divides $(t-1)(t+1)$, which has distinct roots when $\mathrm{char}(F) \neq 2$. So $\sigma$ is diagonalizable and $V \otimes V$ is the sum of its $+1$ and $-1$ eigenspaces, the symmetric part $S^2 V$ and the alternating part $\Lambda^2 V$. The projections onto the eigenspaces are $\tfrac12(1 \pm \sigma)$, and the $\tfrac12$ is the only place where $\mathrm{char}(F) \neq 2$ is used. Every tensor decomposes accordingly, $$v \otimes w = \tfrac{1}{2}(v \otimes w + w \otimes v) + \tfrac{1}{2}(v \otimes w - w \otimes v),$$ where the first piece is symmetric and the second is alternating.
Example. Let $V = F^2$ with basis $e_1, e_2$. Then $V \otimes V$ has basis $\{e_i \otimes e_j\}$ of size $4$. $S^2 V$ has basis $\{e_1^2, e_1 e_2, e_2^2\}$ of size $3$, and $\Lambda^2 V$ has basis $\{e_1 \wedge e_2\}$ of size $1$. The total is $3 + 1 = 4$.
Non-example. In characteristic $2$ the eigenspace decomposition fails. Since $+1 = -1$, $\sigma$ has only one eigenspace, the symmetric tensors, and that isn't all of $V \otimes V$ (for example $e_1 \otimes e_2$ isn't symmetric). So $\sigma$ isn't diagonalizable and there's no natural way to split $V \otimes V$ like this. (The dimensions still add up, so the two sides are isomorphic as vector spaces.)
Remark. Tensor products can vanish. (D&F §10.4) We have $\mathbb{Z}/2 \otimes_\mathbb{Z} \mathbb{Z}/3 = 0$, since the generator $\bar 1 \otimes \bar 1$ satisfies $\bar 1 \otimes \bar 1 = 3(\bar 1 \otimes \bar 1) - 2(\bar 1 \otimes \bar 1) = \bar 1 \otimes \bar 3 - \bar 2 \otimes \bar 1 = 0$. More generally, $\mathbb{Z}/m \otimes_\mathbb{Z} \mathbb{Z}/n \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$ for all positive integers $m, n$. This is because $\mathbb{Z}/m \otimes \mathbb{Z}/n$ is generated by $\bar 1 \otimes \bar 1$, which is killed by both $m$ and $n$, so it's killed by $\gcd(m,n)$. So when the orders are coprime, the tensor product is $0$.
