Hom and tensor products

Def. Tensor product. (D&F §10.4) For a right $R$-module $M$ and left $R$-module $N$, the tensor product $M \otimes_R N$ is the abelian group generated by $m \otimes n$ modulo bilinearity and the balanced relation $mr \otimes n = m \otimes rn$. The ring action "slides" through the tensor, killing any incompatibility between left and right actions. Universal property: $R$-balanced maps $M \times N \to A$ correspond to homomorphisms $M \otimes_R N \to A$.
Example. $\mathbb{Q} \otimes_\mathbb{Z} \mathbb{Q} \cong \mathbb{Q}$. $\mathbb{R} \otimes_\mathbb{Q} \mathbb{R}$ is a huge $\mathbb{R}$-vector space (uncountable dimension).
Non-example (vanishing). $\mathbb{Z}/2 \otimes_\mathbb{Z} \mathbb{Z}/3 = 0$: for any generators $\bar 1 \otimes \bar 1$, we have $\bar 1 \otimes \bar 1 = 3 (\bar 1 \otimes \bar 1) - 2 (\bar 1 \otimes \bar 1) = \bar 1 \otimes \bar 3 - \bar 2 \otimes \bar 1 = 0$.
Rules. Tensor identities (over commutative $R$). (D&F §10.4)
  • $M \otimes_R R \cong M$.
  • $(M \oplus M') \otimes N \cong (M \otimes N) \oplus (M' \otimes N)$ (distributivity over $\oplus$).
  • $M \otimes N \cong N \otimes M$ (commutativity).
  • $(M \otimes N) \otimes P \cong M \otimes (N \otimes P)$ (associativity).
  • $R/I \otimes_R R/J \cong R/(I + J)$.
  • $\mathbb{Z}/m \otimes_\mathbb{Z} \mathbb{Z}/n \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m, n)$.
  • $M \otimes_R N$ inherits scalar multiplication, so for $S \supseteq R$, $S \otimes_R M$ is naturally an $S$-module ("base change").
Computation. $\mathbb{Z}/3 \otimes_\mathbb{Z} \mathbb{Z}/2 \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(3,2) = \mathbb{Z}/1 = 0$. $\mathbb{Z}/4 \otimes_\mathbb{Z} \mathbb{Z}/2 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$.
Prop. (D&F §10.5, Ex. 8) $\mathrm{Hom}_R(R/I, M) \cong \{m \in M : Im = 0\}$, the elements of $M$ killed by $I$. An $R$-linear $\phi\colon R/I \to M$ is determined by $\phi(\bar 1)$, and well-definedness forces every element of $I$ to kill $\phi(\bar 1)$. So $\mathrm{Hom}_\mathbb{Z}(\mathbb{Z}/n, M) \cong M[n] = \{m : nm = 0\}$.
Prop (Adjoint associativity). (D&F §10.5, Thm. 43) $\mathrm{Hom}_R(M \otimes_R N, P) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_R(M, \mathrm{Hom}_R(N, P))$.
Def. Symmetric and exterior powers. (D&F §11.5) $S^2 V = (V \otimes V)/\langle v \otimes w - w \otimes v\rangle$ is the symmetric square and $\Lambda^2 V = (V \otimes V)/\langle v \otimes v\rangle$ is the alternating (exterior) square. If $\dim V = n$ then $\dim S^2 V = \binom{n+1}{2}$ and $\dim \Lambda^2 V = \binom{n}{2}$, so the dimensions sum to $n^2 = \dim V \otimes V$.
Prop. $V \otimes V \cong S^2 V \oplus \Lambda^2 V$ when $\mathrm{char}(F) \neq 2$. (D&F §11.5) The swap $\sigma(v \otimes w) = w \otimes v$ is an involution, $\sigma^2 = 1$, with minimal polynomial dividing $(t-1)(t+1)$, distinct roots once $\mathrm{char}(F) \neq 2$. So $\sigma$ is diagonalizable and $V \otimes V$ is the sum of its $+1$ and $-1$ eigenspaces, the symmetric part $S^2 V$ and the alternating part $\Lambda^2 V$. Project by averaging over the swap, $\tfrac12(1 \pm \sigma)$, where the $\tfrac12$ is the only place $\mathrm{char}(F) \neq 2$ is used. Every tensor splits to match, $$v \otimes w = \tfrac{1}{2}(v \otimes w + w \otimes v) + \tfrac{1}{2}(v \otimes w - w \otimes v),$$ the first piece symmetric, the second alternating.
Example. $V = F^2$ with basis $e_1, e_2$. Then $V \otimes V$ has basis $\{e_i \otimes e_j\}$ of size $4$. $S^2 V$ has basis $\{e_1^2, e_1 e_2, e_2^2\}$ of size $3$, and $\Lambda^2 V$ has basis $\{e_1 \wedge e_2\}$ of size $1$. Total $3 + 1 = 4$.
Non-example. In characteristic $2$ the decomposition fails. Now $v \otimes w + w \otimes v$ and $v \otimes w - w \otimes v$ coincide, so $S^2 V \oplus \Lambda^2 V$ misses elements of $V \otimes V$.
Fun fact. Tensor products can vanish. (D&F §10.4) $\mathbb{Z}/2 \otimes_\mathbb{Z} \mathbb{Z}/3 = 0$: the generators $\bar 1 \otimes \bar 1$ satisfy $\bar 1 \otimes \bar 1 = 3(\bar 1 \otimes \bar 1) - 2(\bar 1 \otimes \bar 1) = \bar 1 \otimes \bar 3 - \bar 2 \otimes \bar 1 = 0$, so they are zero. More generally, $\mathbb{Z}/m \otimes_\mathbb{Z} \mathbb{Z}/n \cong \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m,n)$: the tensor captures the largest cyclic subgroup of both, annihilated by their actions. Coprime torsion annihilates each other.
