Free groups

Def. Free group. (D&F §6.3) The free group $F_n$ on $n$ generators has a universal property: any $f\colon \{x_1, \ldots, x_n\} \to G$ to a group $G$ extends to a unique homomorphism $F_n \to G$. No relations except group axioms. Every group is a quotient of some free group. The elements of $F_n$ are reduced words in the letters $x_i^{\pm 1}$ with no $x_i x_i^{-1}$ adjacencies.
Example. $F_1 \cong \mathbb{Z}$: a single generator with no relations gives an infinite cyclic group. $F_2$ is countable (countable alphabet, finite words), yet it has every countable group as a quotient. Small generating set, but explosive quotient richness.
Non-example. $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ isn't free: all free groups are torsion-free, but $\mathbb{Z}/2$ has torsion. No nontrivial finite group is free.
Thm (Schreier). (D&F §6.3, Thm. 19) Every subgroup of a free group is free.
Fun fact. Schreier index formula. A subgroup of index $k$ in $F_n$ is free of rank $k(n-1) + 1$. For $n = k = 2$: an index-$2$ subgroup of $F_2$ has rank $2(2-1) + 1 = 3$, so $F_3 \hookrightarrow F_2$. A finite-index subgroup of a free group can be larger (more generators) than the whole group. (D&F §6.3)
Computation. Explicit $F_3 \subseteq F_2$: Set $F_2 = \langle a, b \rangle$ and let $H = \ker(F_2 \to \mathbb{Z}/2 : a \mapsto 1, b \mapsto 0)$. So $H$ is the subgroup of words sending $a$ to an odd number of times and $b$ any number of times. Index $[F_2 : H] = 2$, with coset representatives $\{1, a\}$. Schreier rewriting gives three independent free generators of $H$: $b$, $a^2$, $aba^{-1}$. We've built $F_3$ inside $F_2$, proving it abstractly exists without freeness up front.
