Finite fields

Def. Algebraic closure. (D&F §13.4) An algebraic closure $\bar{F}$ of $F$ is an algebraic extension in which every nonconstant polynomial of $F[x]$ splits completely. Equivalently, $\bar{F}$ is algebraic and algebraically closed, the maximal algebraic extension of $F$. Algebraic closures exist (by choice + Zorn) and are unique up to isomorphism over $F$. For any two closures $\bar F, \bar F'$, any isomorphism $\sigma: F \to F'$ lifts to an isomorphism $\bar{F} \to \bar{F}'$.
Thm. Isomorphism extension. (D&F §13.1, §13.4) An embedding $\sigma : F \hookrightarrow \overline{L}$ into an algebraically closed field extends to any algebraic extension $K/F$. Application: every automorphism of an algebraic extension of $F$ lifts to the algebraic closure. For $K/\mathbb{Q}$ Galois, the restriction map $\mathrm{Aut}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}) \to \mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ is surjective.
Example. $\overline{\mathbb{F}_p} = \bigcup_{n \geq 1} \mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ is the algebraic closure of $\mathbb{F}_p$. It is countable (a countable union of finite fields). $\overline{\mathbb{R}} = \mathbb{C}$, the unique algebraic closure of the reals (up to isomorphism).
Non-example. $\overline{\mathbb{Q}} \subsetneq \mathbb{C}$. The complex numbers contain transcendentals like $\pi, e$, which are not algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$. So $\mathbb{C}$ is algebraically closed but not an algebraic closure of $\mathbb{Q}$.
Thm. (D&F §13.5, §14.3) For each prime power $q = p^n$ there exists a unique field $\mathbb{F}_q$ of order $q$ up to isomorphism. $\mathbb{F}_q$ is the splitting field of $x^q - x$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$, where the roots are exactly the $q$ elements of $\mathbb{F}_q$ (since $a^q = a$ for all $a$). Key properties:
  • $\mathbb{F}_q^\times$ is cyclic of order $q - 1$.
  • $\mathbb{F}_{p^m} \subseteq \mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ iff $m \mid n$. Subfields correspond bijectively to divisors of $n$.
  • $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{p^n}/\mathbb{F}_p) \cong \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, generated by Frobenius $\sigma\colon \alpha \mapsto \alpha^p$.
  • An irreducible $f \in \mathbb{F}_p[x]$ of degree $d$ divides $x^{p^n} - x$ iff $d \mid n$.
Computation. Factor $x^9 - x$ over $\mathbb{F}_3$. Note $9 = 3^2$. Roots of $x^9 - x$ are elements $a \in \mathbb{F}_{3^2}$. Irreducible factors are exactly the irreducibles of degree $d$ where $d \mid 2$, so degrees $1$ or $2$. Degree $1$: three linear factors $x, x-1, x+1$ from $\mathbb{F}_3$. Degree $2$: count is $\frac{3^2 - 3}{2} = 3$ irreducibles, namely $x^2 + 1, x^2 + x + 2, x^2 + 2x + 2$. Thus $x^9 - x = x(x-1)(x+1)(x^2 + 1)(x^2 + x + 2)(x^2 + 2x + 2)$.
Remark. Aut of algebraic closure of a finite field. (D&F §14.3, §14.9) An automorphism of $\overline{\mathbb{F}_p} = \bigcup_n \mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ is determined by its restrictions to the finite subfields, and any choices that agree on common subfields assemble into an automorphism of the union. Along the chain $\mathbb{F}_{p^2} \subset \mathbb{F}_{p^4} \subset \mathbb{F}_{p^8} \subset \cdots$ the automorphisms of $\mathbb{F}_{p^{2^k}}$ are the $2^k$ powers of Frobenius, and each choice extends to the next field in exactly two ways. So the compatible chains of choices match the infinite binary sequences, and there are uncountably many. Each one is an automorphism of $\bigcup_k \mathbb{F}_{p^{2^k}}$ and extends to $\overline{\mathbb{F}_p}$ by the isomorphism extension theorem above, distinct ones to distinct ones. Hence $\overline{\mathbb{F}_p}$ is countable while $\mathrm{Aut}(\overline{\mathbb{F}_p}/\mathbb{F}_p)$ is uncountable, and the powers of Frobenius account for only a countable subgroup.
